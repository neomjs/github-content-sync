Resolves #267

The two navigation corrections from the operator's #10 ruling. The keeper nav keeps its six destinations, routes and order, but shows only the icons: upright, at the icon scale, each tab speaking its label as a tooltip. The label stays the tab's accessible name, clipped rather than removed. On the right rail, each item rests on its own panel-ground chip, so the engine's 2px gap reads as cockpit ground between chips instead of a word space inside a label. The three states take three grounds (panel, panel-2 on hover, line with the signal ink when revealed), so hovered and open never share a picture. Both changes are token values and app-side rules; nothing in the engine changes.

Evidence: L3 (the icon-rail contract asserted in a real browser; the rail and nav rendered and compared in both skins) → L3 required (AC-1/AC-2 need a rendered browser). Residual: none.

**Does the active chip outrank the resting one? Yes, in both skins.** WCAG contrast of each rail ground against the cockpit ground (`--fm-ground`), from the token values in `theme-neo-{dark,light}/apps/agentos/Viewport.scss`:

| skin | resting `--fm-panel` | hover `--fm-panel-2` | active `--fm-line` | active vs resting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dark (lighter = raised) | 1.106:1 | 1.195:1 | **1.433:1** | 1.295:1 |

Active is the strongest ground in both, before the SIGNAL ink it also carries. The light-skin hover sits closer to the ground than resting does (1.037 vs 1.094), a quiet off-white hover. That is a register choice, not an inversion, and it is noted here for the design pass.

This PR's `transform: none; margin: 0` on the glyph undoes exactly those two.

### 🧠 Graph Ingestion Notes

*   **`[RETROSPECTIVE]`**: **A gap and a word space are the same shape, and only one of them is legible.** The engine gives rail tabs a 2px gap; on a transparent resting ground that gap is the same visual object as the space inside a rotated label, so the rail could not be *counted* without hovering. The fix did not add spacing, a divider, or a hover affordance — it gave the resting state a ground, and the pre-existing gap became cockpit floor between chips. That is the transferable shape: when two adjacent visual objects are indistinguishable, the answer is usually to make one of them a surface, not to change the geometry between them. The same move appears on the nav, where the labels stopped being painted rather than being removed. Both are *removals of ambiguity* rather than additions of affordance, which is why the diff is 60 lines and not 600.
