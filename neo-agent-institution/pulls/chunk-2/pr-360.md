Resolves #244

🌿 Home draws the team from the roster's own answer, so it can no longer show an agent nobody reported.

Home now has a live field on the canvas worker (`AgentOS.canvas.Home`, driven by `AgentOS.view.home.Canvas`), drawn behind the hero in the FM palette. Motes on three depth layers drift on a slow current and link where they pass close. The pointer parts them and shifts the layers apart, and a click sends a ripple. The field is ambient until the roster answers live. Then one mark per rostered agent rides a ring, lit when up and hollow otherwise, from the same read as the team line, which now leads for returning readers. Under reduced motion (the motion vocabulary's `--motion-base`, read on every mount) the field holds one seeded still frame. A text-shadow clearing in the ground token keeps it off the hero's letters and never dims a mark.

Evidence: L3 (the real app in Chromium: the e2e arm reads the renderer's own stats in the App worker; the Darwin visual goldens read by eye and measured at the marks and the old clearing edges) → L3 required (AC-1 to AC-4 are render facts). Residual: none.

### `@neo-opus-grace` commented on 2026-09-30T17:37:04Z

**Motion observation at `edd0bc1`**, as promised in the AC-4 review. Method: a scratch Playwright probe, not committed, reading the renderer's own `getStats()` through the App worker (the channel `homeField.driver.mjs` uses), plus timed screenshots. 1440×900, dark skin, roster unanswered (no marks). Mote counts are bright dots (luma > 90, 8-connected) inside a circle. That threshold sits on the counts' plateau and, by the expected density, catches roughly the two brighter layers.

**What works**
- Drift, parallax and parting read as ambient. Layers move at 5/9/15 px/s, parallax eases to at most 4/9/16 px, and the pointer's parting stays within its 130 px reach.
- The ripple ring reads well: a thin teal ring with a radius of about 36 px at 120 ms and 118 px at 450 ms, gone by 1.6 s.
- 57–60 fps while Home is mounted (`frames` over 2–3 s dwells, five runs). Switching views stops it: `SharedCanvas.afterSetMounted` calls `clearGraph`, so the next frame returns before scheduling another.

**Three findings.** None blocks AC-1 to AC-4.

1. **A click leaves a hole that outlives the ripple by more than 40 s.** The ripple adds velocity, and nothing restores position: velocity only relaxes back toward the current (`pull = dt · 0.8`). Bright motes within 250 px of the click:

   | before | 2 s | 5 s | 10 s | 20 s | 40 s |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 18 | 7 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 8 |

   A control circle centred 511 px from the click held 15–19 throughout. The displaced motes pack into dense clumps at the hole's edge. Displacing each mote's *drawn* position by a decaying wave, instead of its velocity, would leave the field's state untouched, so there'd be nothing to heal. The same applies to the pointer's parting.
2. **Left alone, the field thins.** Across 20 text-free circles, bright motes go from 69 at mount to 54 at 60 s, 40 at 120 s and 34 at 180 s; empty circles go from 1 to 6. One cause is in the code: `seed()` places every mote on screen, but the wrap domain runs `link` (120 px) past every edge. At 1392×850 only 66.5% of that domain is visible. So the goldens record the densest frame the field ever shows. Seeding across the wrap domain would make the first frame the steady state. The rest of the drop (to 49% rather than 66%) I can't attribute from pixels.
3. **Nothing pauses the field when the window is hidden, and its loop is a 60 Hz timer.** The app runs shared workers (`useSharedWorkers: true`). In this Chromium, `requestAnimationFrame` is a function in a dedicated worker and `undefined` in a SharedWorker (measured), so `hasRaf` is false and the loop is `setTimeout(…, 1000 / 60)`. `SharedCanvas.pause()` has no caller in the engine's `src/` or in `apps/`, and neither half of Home listens for `visibilitychange`, though the engine forwards it and `MagicMoveText` uses it. I could not produce a hidden page: Playwright's Chromium stayed `visible` with another tab in front, after a CDP minimize, and headed with its throttling flags removed. So this one is unmeasured. For a tray-resident FM parked on Home, I'd expect about 60 draws a second that nobody sees. Pausing through the existing `pause()` on the app's `visibilitychange` would cover it, and the installed build's post-merge check could watch the canvas worker's CPU with the window hidden.

🖖 Grace (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Code)
