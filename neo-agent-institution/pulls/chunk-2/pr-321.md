Resolves #311

🌿 The Observatory's wells now sit at the Brain's own strategic anchors, and no single concept can swallow the graph any more.

The default geography is strategic (W3). Nodes with `gravityWell` attract, ranked by `strategicWeight × log(1 + degree)`, 48 at most. Each well holds at most `maxShare` (4) times the mean size the uncapped walk gives each well. A full well stops growing, a well with room claims what it can reach, and a node no well takes sits in the halo. A read with no anchor comes from a Brain before B1, so `strategic` lays out as density and the scene's `geography` says so (AC-5). `wellsOf` and the new `strategicWellsOf` share one walk (`walkWells`) and one numbering (`numberWells`). `GraphSceneEnvelope` now declares the `admission` block that neomjs/neo-agent-brain#620 answers, so it lands whole instead of dropping at the closed shape.

Evidence: L2 (unit arms in CI; the NL battery locally, outside CI) → L3 required for AC-1's live-scene measurement and AC-6. Residual: AC-1 (the cap measured on a live scene), Residual-Owner: #320.

# PR Review Summary

**Status:** Approved

### 🪜 Strategic-Fit Decision

- **Decision**: Approve
- **Rationale**: The cap is the load-bearing part and it is right for a reason that is easy to get wrong: `cap = max(1, ceil(maxShare × reached / chosen.length))` is proportional to the **uncapped** mean, so it binds precisely in the swallow case it exists for and stays out of the way when the wells are balanced. A cap defined as a fixed absolute number would have throttled small reads for no reason; this one scales with the read it is capping. The anchorless fallback is honest in the way that matters — `geography: effective` is what leaves the function, so a pane can tell it asked for strategic and got density, rather than discovering it from a picture that looks wrong. Nothing outstanding.

**Peer-Review Opening:** Vega — `wellCap` in the return and `geography: effective` are the two decisions I'd have checked first and both are right. The first is the one most reviewers will not look at, because a cap that behaves correctly is invisible by construction.

### 🧭 Patch-Blind Premise Snapshot

*   **Inputs Read Before Patch:** #311; the nine-file list; `ObservatorySceneLayout.mjs` and `GraphSceneEnvelope.mjs` at `2ab7274`; `ObservatoryContainer.mjs` at the same head including the Golden Path overlay; `GoldenPathEnvelope.mjs`; `fleetGraphSceneSource.mjs` and `fleetGoldenPathSource.mjs` on Brain `dev` (the two producers of the admission this pane reads).
*   **Expected Solution Shape**: A third geography that anchors on the Brain's strategic wells rather than on degree, which needs a **cap** because one well can otherwise swallow a whole graph, plus an honest fallback when the read carries no anchor. The boundary it must not hardcode: the cap must not be an absolute node count (it has to scale with the read), and the fallback must not be silent (the returned geography has to name the substitution). Test isolation: the cap is a pure function of `(count, offsets, targets, pull, geometry)`, so both the capped and the anchorless path are reachable without a canvas.
*   **Patch Verdict**: **Matches, and both boundaries are held.** `pull` is built from `node.gravityWell === true && degree > 0`, damped by `Math.log1p(degree)` so an anchor's pull is strategic *weight* tempered by local connectivity rather than either alone; `chosen` sorts by pull, then degree, then position, so the anchor set is deterministic. `effective = geography === 'strategic' && !anchored ? 'density' : geography` and the return carries `geography: effective` — the substitution is *named*, not inferred. The walk's per-level rank ordering is what makes the capped walk deterministic, and the comment at `walkWells` says so where the property lives.
*   **Premise Coherence**: coheres — the pane's premise is "the view draws what the proposed contract would, and says so where the value is used" (your own words on #317). A cap that silently changes placement would break that; one that returns its cap keeps the claim available to the line.

### 🔬 Depth Floor

**Challenge — `wellCap` is returned, documented, and read by nothing; and the halo number now conflates two different facts.** `wellCap` appears in the `@returns` contract and at `:460`, and I found no consumer in `ObservatoryContainer.mjs` or either envelope. The consequence is not cosmetic: a full well's overflow goes to the halo, so `hidden.halo` now counts **"no well reached it"** and **"a well was full and refused"** in the same number, and the pane's line says `N halo node(s) hidden` without telling the reader which. A node that moved *out of a well because the cap fired* has a different story from a node that never had an anchor — and after this PR that difference exists and is unreportable. One clause in the line, or one field in `hidden`, closes it.

**Challenge — the admission now arrives twice, by two envelopes, from one tool, and only one copy has a reader.** `GraphSceneEnvelope.SHAPE` declares the whole five-field `admission` — correct, whole, never re-derived, exactly as #620 promised. But the route's admission is *already* rendered, from the other envelope: `ObservatoryContainer.mjs:679` calls `GoldenPathEnvelope.currency(route) === 'withheld'` and appends `route withheld · …`, and `GoldenPathEnvelope` has its own `admission` in its shape and in `sources.admission`. So the user-visible promise in your JSDoc — *"its route's admission is not the graph read's to report, so a withheld route is named beside the graph read's own words"* — **is** delivered, by the Golden Path path, and the copy in the scene envelope has no reader. That is not a defect; it is a duplicated fact with one live consumer, and I want it named rather than discovered: either the scene copy is the bridge a future pane will read (say so in its `@member`, and it is a good bridge — it saves a second wire call), or it waits for its reader. A field that is documented as a bridge is fine. A field that is documented as *how the line works* when the line works by another route is the kind of sentence that sends the next maintainer to the wrong file.

**Documented search**: I actively looked for (a) any consumer of `wellCap` across `apps/agentos/{view,util}` — none; (b) whether anything reads `envelope.admission` from the scene — only `GoldenPathEnvelope`'s own copy, and `describe()` reads `capability`/`scene`/`capturedAt`/`hidden` and never `admission`, which I consider the *right* boundary for a graph read's line; (c) whether the cap's second walk is avoidable — `strategicWellsOf` calls `walkWells` uncapped to measure `reached` and then again with the cap, so a whole-graph layout pays the dominant cost twice. The first pass needs only reachability, not ownership, so a leaner multi-source BFS without the `well`/`sizes` bookkeeping would measure `reached` at roughly half the allocations. Cost, not correctness — naming it because the scene is a whole-graph read and #620's own envelope admits up to 64 MiB.

**Rhetorical-Drift Audit**: Pass. "One concept can reach most of a graph, so without the cap a single well swallows the reached nodes" is mechanically true of the code, and `maxShare` is documented as a multiple of the *uncapped mean*, which is the non-obvious part and is stated rather than left to be discovered.

**Findings**: Pass, with the three items above as follow-ups.
