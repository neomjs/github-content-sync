Resolves #1349

🌿 *A transport session that saved nothing can no longer start a summary pass.*

`addMemory` now records its session in-process after the durable WAL append. `Server.onSessionClosed` queues the `SummarizationJobs.pending` marker only for a recorded session, and consumes the record. Sessions that wrote nothing make up 87% of the plane's markers (28,488 `failed` against 4,128 `completed`), and they no longer start a pass whose drift scan reads ~170 MB from Chroma. A restart loses the record; the periodic drift sweep (10 min) stays the backstop.

Evidence: L1 (unit specs plus a mutation run) → L3 required for the post-merge pass-rate reading (a plane running this head). Residual: AC3 (the post-merge pass-rate reading), Residual-Owner: #1350.

### 🔬 Depth Floor

- **Challenge**: Answering your design question (*"Is the in-process record the right home, or would you key it elsewhere?"*): `SessionService.writtenSessionIds` is the right home, with one non-blocking lifecycle nuance worth noting. When a caller passes an explicit `sessionId` to `add_memory({ sessionId })` over Streamable-HTTP (where `TransportService.mjs:298, 309` generates a distinct transport UUID `id` for `mcp-session-id` and passes `id` to `onSessionClosed(id)`), or when `add_memory` runs in `stdio` mode where `onSessionClosed` never fires, `noteSessionWrite(sessionId)` inserts the caller's `sessionId` into `writtenSessionIds`, whereas `onSessionClosed(id)` queries `takeSessionWrite(id)` with the transport UUID.
  1. Behaviorally, returning `false` for `takeSessionWrite(id)` is **correct**: the memories live under the caller's explicit `sessionId`, not the transport UUID `id` (and a multi-turn conversation sharing one explicit `sessionId` across multiple HTTP transport connections should be summarized by the drift sweep after turn/wake activity ends, not when an intermediate transport disconnects).
  2. In-memory footprint: because `takeSessionWrite` is only called with transport UUIDs, explicitly-supplied `sessionId` strings that differ from the transport `mcp-session-id` remain in `writtenSessionIds` until process restart. Since a 10-seat plane mints at most a few dozen distinct conversation `sessionId` UUIDs per day (~1–2 KB total), this is completely benign in practice, and does not warrant keying on `RequestContextService.getSessionId()`.
