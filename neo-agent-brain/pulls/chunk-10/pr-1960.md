Resolves #1959

Memory Core's in-memory graph cache gets a bound. The live mc-server held 113k nodes and 491k edges, growing about 53k edges an hour toward the heap ceiling ([#1843 measurements](https://github.com/neomjs/neo-agent-brain/issues/1843#issuecomment-7224583482)).

`Database`'s collector:
- now tracks every node that enters the cache, through the nodes Store's `mutate` event. Before, it tracked only vicinity anchors.
- past `maxGraphNodes`, evicts the least recently used nodes down to 80 % of the bound.
- removes their incident edges and clears both ends' vicinity marks, so a later `getAdjacentNodes` reloads a whole vicinity rather than answering from part of one.
- never writes to storage and waits while a transaction runs.
- runs on the macrotask after `requestGarbageCollection`, so a caller that loads and then reads or iterates the cache in one synchronous flow never sees an eviction in between.

Memory Core's `GraphService` sets the bound from `memoryService.graphCacheMaxNodes` (60,000, `NEO_MC_GRAPH_CACHE_MAX_NODES`). It pins `GRAPH_CACHE_PINS`, the populations other services read straight from the cache: `WAKE_SUBSCRIPTION` nodes, and PermissionService's grant edges with their endpoints. `maxGraphNodes: null` keeps every other consumer as it was.

Evidence: L2 (unit specs on a real SQLite store: eviction, reload, storage rows, pins, deferral, the leaf wiring) → L3 required for AC-4 (a 24 h heap read on the cut plane). Residual: AC-4, Residual-Owner: #1843.

## Deltas from ticket
- **Eviction is deferred to a macrotask.** `MailboxService`'s trust-lift iterates `db.edges.items` while calling `getAdjacentNodes` inside the loop (`:3837`), so a synchronous collector would mutate the array it walks.
- **The existing LRU arm in `GraphService.spec.mjs` now encodes the new contract.** Its old form assumed the synchronous, evict-one behavior. Under the new one, the collector runs on the next macrotask and trims to 80 %. At its end the arm restores the leaf's bound, not `null`.
- **Reader audit:**
  - Cold-cache argument: a reader that reads a node without loading it already meets a miss after boot, so eviction adds nothing new for it. A node read after its own load in the same synchronous flow is never evicted in between, because the collector is deferred.
  - Readers that treat the cache as the population, so pins were needed:
    - `WakeSubscriptionService` scans `db.nodes.items` for `WAKE_SUBSCRIPTION` at `:510`, `:1965` and `:3031`.
    - `PermissionService` scans `db.edges.items` for grants at `:104` and `:206`.
  - The remaining full scans:
    - `GraphService:1655` and `:1737` are no-SQLite fallbacks.
    - `GraphService:2455`, the frontier edge, behaves as it does on a cold cache.
    - `MailboxService:6154` follows `getAdjacentNodes(messageId, 'both')` in the same flow.
- **Out of scope, noted on #1959:** both pinned services already miss on a cold cache, because neither loads its population. Moving them to SQL would retire the pins.

### 🔬 Depth Floor

- **Challenge**: Non-blocking edge case in `Database#requestGarbageCollection` (`ai/graph/Database.mjs:311-323`) + `runGarbageCollector` (`:234`): if the queued `setImmediate` callback fires while `me.isExecutingTransaction === true`, `gcScheduled` resets to `false` and `runGarbageCollector()` returns early without re-arming from `endTransaction()`. Under `mc-server` request/projection traffic the next `getAdjacentNodes()` or `addNode()` call observes `lastAccessMap.size > maxGraphNodes` and re-schedules collection, keeping any overshoot transient; if long idle windows after large transactions ever matter, `endTransaction()` can invoke `this.requestGarbageCollection()`. Second non-blocking note: pinned nodes/endpoints remain in `lastAccessMap` while skipped as victims, which is negligible at current `WAKE_SUBSCRIPTION` / permission-edge cardinality (~dozens vs `60,000`) and disappears once those two services move their population scans to SQL.
