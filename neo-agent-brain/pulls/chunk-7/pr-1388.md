`matchDeferencePhrase` read every `your call` outside its four carves as a handback. Four live firings recorded on #124 since September were none of the kind: a decision dated by a duration ("about three hours after your call"), one negated or contrasted away ("my decision rather than your call"), one the author attributed to their own past speech ("the first was telling you the approval was your call when `maintain: true` made it mine"), and one quoted on a Markdown blockquote line. Each class gets an adjacency-anchored exemption — a duration before `after|since` as one more `CITATION_ANCHOR` form judged through the existing bridge; a negator or contrast marker with at most one article or adverb between it and the phrase; a first-person speech attribution in the same clause with the copula right before the phrase; blockquote lines stripped as quoted mentions — and the fair hits recorded beside them keep firing. `DEFERENCE_PHRASES`, the operator-dialogue carve and `stopHookDecision.mjs` are unchanged.

### 🔬 Depth Floor

- **Challenge**:
  1. **Tense-agnostic duration branch in `CITATION_ANCHOR` (`ai/scripts/lifecycle/deferencePhraseMatch.mjs:23-29,117-125`):** `CITATION_ANCHOR` exempts `(?:\d+|one|two|three|four|five|six|seven|eight|nine|ten|a\s+few|several)\s+(?:seconds?|minutes?|hours?|days?|turns?)\s*$` immediately preceding `after|since` (`isCitationBridge`), which cleanly silences `three hours after your call` (`#124` `6427021530`). Because the duration branch keys on the duration phrase rather than past-tense morphology, a hypothetical future-tense deferral carrying an explicit duration before `after your call` (e.g., `"I can wait two hours after your call"`) would also match `CITATION_ANCHOR` unless caught by a second phrase (`let me know` / `would you like me to`). Given zero such future-tense duration deferrals in `#124`'s 31-day corpus, keeping the regex small and linear is the right trade-off today; if `#124` ever records one, anchoring against preceding modal/future markers (`\b(?:will|'ll|can)\s+\w+\s+`) is the surgical follow-up.
  2. **Mandatory addressee pronoun in `FIRST_PERSON_ATTRIBUTION` (`ai/scripts/lifecycle/deferencePhraseMatch.mjs:46-47`):** `/\b(?:i\s+(?:told|said|wrote|asked)|(?:was\s+|kept\s+)?telling)\s+(?:you|him|her|them)\b[^.!?\n]*\b(?:was|is)\s*$/` requires `you|him|her|them` immediately after the reporting verb, so `I told you that X was your call` and `kept telling you the merge was your call` are exempted while pronoun-less `I wrote that the merge was your call` is not (unless `wrote` is preceded by a `#123` artifact anchor). That tight pronoun requirement prevents over-carving forward-looking `I said ... is your call` clauses.
