Resolves #974

🌿 A daily bundle no longer spends ten gigabytes writing four thousand decimals per row as text: a payload is read by its extension, and a bundle retained from before today and one written at the head come back line for line.

Every exported payload (kb, mc, graph) now streams through brotli at quality 4 to `.jsonl.br`. One module, `ai/services/shared/bundlePayload.mjs`, owns the encoding decision: the extension vocabulary (`.jsonl`, `.jsonl.br`, `.jsonl.gz`), the read side (`openBundlePayload` / `closeBundlePayload`) and the write side (`createBundlePayloadWriteStream` / `endBundlePayload`). Every reader in the lane goes through it — `restore.mjs`, `restoreReceipts.mjs`, the integrity line count and retention classifier in `backup.mjs`, `backupCorruptionTimeline.mjs`, and the two import gates (`graphJsonlImport.mjs`, `vectorJsonlSourceValidation.mjs`) — so a retained `.jsonl` bundle and a new `.jsonl.br` one share one path and need no migration. `bundle-meta.json` records `payloadEncoding` per exported subsystem as a receipt, never as a reader input. The flat copies (`concepts/`, `trajectories/`, `mailbox/`, `ledgers/`) stay `.jsonl`: verbatim copies restored verbatim, under 1 % of the bytes. Today's real bundle re-encoded through the helper: 10.14 GB → 3.92 GB (2.59×) in 115 s.

Evidence: L3 (today's 10.14 GB bundle re-encoded through the helper, validated by `validateBundle` with an integrity pass on kb/mc/graph, and a 2,000-row slice restored into a scratch Chroma through the receipt path) → L4 required for AC-3 (the first daily bundle the cut plane writes). Residual: AC-3, Residual-Owner: #969.

## Deltas from ticket
- The helper lives in `ai/services/shared/` beside `atomicFileWrite.mjs`, not in `ai/scripts/maintenance/`: both services and the scripts import it (intake comment on #974).
- Compression covers the three exported substrates; the flat copies stay bare (AC-1 amended at intake).
- `closeBundlePayload` is new: a composed reader destroyed before its own close reports that destroy as an `AbortError`, and the readers' early-exit `rl.input.destroy()` crashed the next unit test a tick later. The helper owns the close; the spec that found it is in `bundlePayload.spec.mjs`.
- `endBundlePayload` settles on `'close'` through `stream/promises`: the composed writer calls `end()`'s callback before a deferred open error surfaces, so the exporters' old `new Promise(resolve => stream.end(resolve))` could return a receipt for a failed export (spec: a writer whose directory does not exist).
- Fix 2 (float32 embeddings) is not taken: its reader change is not as small as its writer change. Fix 3 (fp16) and retention stay out of scope as the ticket says.
- Decayed ticket ids in touched comments became prose.
