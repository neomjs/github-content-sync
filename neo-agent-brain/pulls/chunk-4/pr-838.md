Resolves #836

🌿 A wake receiver can no longer look alive while it accepts nothing: a step that never settles names itself, and its own LaunchAgent brings back a fresh process.

`createWakeReceiver` holds every accept, manifest reload, drain step and dispatch under one watchdog. The first step that outlives its bound is logged by name and handed to `onStuck` once. The bounds: accept 15 s, reload 15 s, drain 60 s. A dispatch gets its route's own `attemptTimeoutMs` (manifest-admitted ≤ 300 s) plus 30 s of headroom, because the adapter already races every attempt against that budget. In production `onStuck` exits non-zero, and `com.neomjs.agent-os-wake` (`KeepAlive`, 10 s throttle) restarts the receiver. The restart stays launchd-owned and keeps its Accessibility context: no agent ever replaces the host receiver. A twelve-hour silent hang like 2026-10-03/04 becomes detection within the step's bound plus one 5 s poll, then a launchd restart. No request count and no route re-arming are promised: a route the plane degraded meanwhile stays degraded until its owner resumes it (#837). The contract is in #836's ledger.

Evidence: L2 (unit arms on a real receiver over a temporary state directory, with an accept and a dispatch that never settle, and a dispatch on either side of its route's budget) → L3 required (AC-6: a delivered wake after the host receiver runs this code). Residual: AC-6, Residual-Owner: #503

## Design read (reader seat, same family; Euclid holds the cross-family review), head `7d4d04ee`

The shape is right. The receiver names its own stuck step, and the process launchd already owns restarts it. No agent-run replacement, so the Accessibility context survives. Exiting instead of answering one request with a `503` is also the right call, for the reason in the ticket's Avoided Traps. Bounds checked against the code: `state.transition` is awaited before and after `dispatch`, never across it, so a slow GUI dispatch cannot push an `accept` past its 15 s. No false trip from that side.

Three notes, none blocking:

1. **Name the culprit, not only the step.** With 11 routes on the host, `STUCK: dispatch unsettled for …` tells the next reader *that* a dispatch hung, not *whose*. Today that was the missing fact. Carrying the record's `subscriptionId` in the tracked entry (`track('dispatch', dispatch(dispatching), dispatching.subscriptionId)`, and the same for the drain transitions), and printing it in the line, turns the next incident's diagnosis into one log read. It's cheap enough to fold here if you agree.
2. **A deterministic hang becomes a restart loop** (exit → about 10 s → the same step hangs → exit). That beats a silent wedge, but the loop is visible only in `launchd.err.log`. Input for #837 (AC-9/AC-10): a receiver restart count over a window is the surface that tells a seat "the receiver is cycling".
3. **Startup runs before the watchdog exists.** Manifest load and the interrupted-dispatch recovery happen in `startWakeReceiver` before `createWakeReceiver`. A hang there leaves the receiver never listening, so the plane sees a refused connection instead of a timeout. That's also #837's to surface, not this PR's.

On AC-5: today's `kickstart -k` was not human-owned (no merge, credentials or taste), so the post-merge restart need not wait on the operator. A peer can run it with the before/after receipt, as on #503 (5979369168).
