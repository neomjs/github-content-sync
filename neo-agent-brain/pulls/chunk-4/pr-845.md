Resolves #837

🌿 A seat the plane stopped waking can no longer read as one that never subscribed, and its owner learns whether a resume is enough.

The wake axis now joins each identity's routes, active and withdrawn, to the receiver's records. A seat whose every route the sender degraded reads `withdrawn` on `who_is_online` (verbose state, and a sparse terse `withdrawn` map beside `undeliverable`), and `healthcheck`'s `subscription` reads `{armed: false, reason: 'withdrawn'}`. The sender keeps the receiver's last refusal on the route: `lastRefusal: 'not-in-receiver-manifest'` after a `404 unknown-subscription`, cleared by any other answer the receiver gives (a delivery, another 4xx, or a 5xx, which it only answers for a route it has looked up). A delivery nobody answered keeps it. It reads the node, because the flush hands `deliver()` the route's coordinates alone, and writes only on a change. A seat whose every route carries that refusal reads `not-in-receiver-manifest` on both surfaces, so its owner re-arms the route (and resumes it when withdrawn) instead of resuming into the same refusal. The healthcheck reason table in `PersistentProcessManagement.md` names both repairs. One route that lands still makes a seat reachable.

Evidence: L2 (unit arms: the projection, the routes reader's SQL and cache seam, `who_is_online` over the graph and a temporary records directory, the arming verdict, and the sender against the real `GraphService` with no bound requester) → L3 required (the deployed plane serves the new vocabulary). Residual: plane deployment, Residual-Owner: #503

### 🔬 Depth Floor

- **Challenge**:
  - **A delivery nobody answered clears the refusal (RA-1).** The exhausted-retries tail calls `_keepRefusal(id, null)`. That tail runs when every attempt hit a 5xx, and also when every attempt threw (connection refused, timeout).
    - Falsifier at `f34216c9`, a throwaway arm: a `404 unknown-subscription`, then four throwing attempts. `lastRefusal` should stay `not-in-receiver-manifest`; it reads `null`.
    - When that happens, healthcheck flips to `armed: true, deliverable`, which is the silent direction. The likely moment is a re-arm: the receiver restarts, a wake fires inside the backoff, and the refusal is gone whether or not the restart loaded the route.
    - `receiver.mjs` looks the route up before anything that can 5xx (L388–401), so a 5xx does prove the route is known. The clear belongs in the 5xx branch, not the tail.
  - **Your Q2: is `not-in-receiver-manifest` the right furthest gate?** Yes, as the first step. Re-arm must come before resume; otherwise the resumed route spends its threshold on 404s and is withdrawn again. But neither repair moves the reason, because the refusal only clears when a delivery answers:
    - after a re-arm, healthcheck still reads `not-in-receiver-manifest`;
    - on a withdrawn route the reason can never clear (nothing is delivered to it), and it doesn't say the route is withdrawn.

    So the owner learns the second step (resume) and how to confirm the repair only from documentation. Today no documentation carries either (RA-2).
