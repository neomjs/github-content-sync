Resolves #557

🌿 The health flag answers one question again, whether the server serves, and what the operator should look at while it does stops being spoken as if it did not.

The operator's ruling: `degraded` meant a maintenance lane behind schedule while the Memory Core served every tool, and the flag should say the server is healthy and name the rest elsewhere. Four sites folded maintenance posture into the serving verdict: the service memory-ceiling fold and the heavy-maintenance starvation fold in `HealthService.mjs`, and the backup, corpus-projection and provider-admission signals at the tool boundary in `toolService.mjs`. Each now records an advisory through one exported helper, `noteHealthAdvisory`: `posture` moves `clear` → `attention`, `advisories[]` gains an entry naming its `axis`, `state` and the axis's own reason codes, and the all-clear line withdraws; `status` is untouched. The two serving impairments keep their verdicts as before: a failed or stale embedding canary and a stalled WAL embed drain read `degraded`, an unreachable database or an unready startup dependency `unhealthy`. The base payload declares `posture: 'clear'` and `advisories: []`, the composed surface carries them through, and `HealthCheckResponse` in the openapi documents `status` as the serving verdict and the two new fields with their axis vocabulary.

Evidence: L2 (unit arms across the two folds, the tool-boundary composition and the schema completeness arm; the flipped arms red at dev) → L4 required (the deployed plane's payload, AC-1's live sample). Residual: AC-1's plane sample, Residual-Owner: #64.
