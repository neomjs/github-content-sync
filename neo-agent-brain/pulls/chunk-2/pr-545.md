Resolves #546
Refs #533

Ships the Brain side of the bounded graph-scene feed: a `fleetGraphScene` read-observe method that
projects a bounded, origin-qualified neighbourhood around the viewer's live computed route, plus its
wire, bridge method, policy rows, `devFleetServer` wiring, and spec. The scene always reports the
budget it was read under and whether it is whole, because a viewer that cannot tell a capped
neighbourhood from a complete one renders a slice as though it were the graph.

**#546 is the close target, not #533, and that is deliberate.** #533 spans two repositories: this
feed plus a post-merge receipt on the pane side. Closing it here would assert a cross-repo AC this
repo cannot deliver, and the shared `pr-body` baseline enforces a real close target — so the
Brain-side slice has its own leaf and #533 stays open carrying AC-4.

**The two cuts are deliberately not the same cut.** A budget cut reports `completeness: 'truncated'`
with the budget that caused it. A scope cut — a neighbour the reader may not see — reports
`'complete'`, and the withheld row leaves with every edge touching it so nothing dangles. Reporting a
permission as a budget would tell the viewer its sight is a limit when it is a policy.

## Deltas from ticket

Three places where the implementation is deliberately narrower or more honest than the sketch, each
forced by a property of the graph operations rather than by preference:

- **An emitted edge is adjacency presence `{from, to}` and carries no relation label.** The neighbour
  projection drops every edge property except `weight`, so a `type` read through that seam is
  *absent* data, not weak data. An earlier draft defaulted it, which would have rendered every real
  edge in the cockpit as the same invented relation. A future feed needing relation semantics reads
  raw edge rows.
- **`completeness` is scoped to the RLS-filtered projection, not the raw graph.** Edge-RLS is a
  surface distinct from node-RLS — an edge between two visible nodes can itself be withheld — so
  "complete" means "everything the projection would show this reader". The pane must not read it as
  "everything that exists".
- **v1 declares no continuation token.** Budgets are mandatory and the envelope's `snapshotId` is the
  identity a future resumable read would bind; a token without that binding would make two reads of
  one snapshot look like two snapshots.

## Test Evidence

**CI found a defect that 31 local arms all passed, and the reason is the useful part.**
`readGraphScene` collected nodes from the adjacency it walked and never handed the links to the
projector, so every read returned `edges: []` — a 3D graph with no links. It survived because every
edge assertion called `projectNeighbourhood` **directly**, and two arms asserted `scene.edges` was
empty, which an edge-less feed satisfies *for the wrong reason*. That is the same shape as a budget
arm kept green earlier by `toBeLessThanOrEqual(4)` against an empty scene: an assertion that cannot
distinguish "the mechanism removed it" from "the mechanism never produced it".

The fix records each hop as a link **before** the already-seen check, so a hop back to a node already
in the scene survives — keying it off `seen` would silently delete every cycle and cross-link. The
new arm reaches edges *through the source* at depth 2, because at depth 1 the neighbours are
discovered but never resolved, so they are legitimately absent and their links correctly drop as
dangling. Local receipt is a node-module instrument (**32 arms, all passing**), used because the
Brain-tier packages are absent at this seat, so `playwright.config.unit.mjs` skips the `unit-brain`
project and the fleet specs do not execute locally at all — a seat condition, not a property of
playwright, and correctable with `npm ci` plus `npm rebuild better-sqlite3`. Separately, the probe
deliberately stops short of the bridge and wire arms: those reach `neo.mjs`, which needs a real `Neo`
namespace, so those two arms are CI-only here rather than locally unrunnable.

Three further defects in this work's own arms were found by running them rather than trusting them,
each now a pinned assertion rather than a comment:

- A budget arm seeded `pr-101` against a graph of `issue-3xx` rows, so the walk resolved nothing —
  and `toBeLessThanOrEqual(4)`, which an *empty* scene satisfies, kept it green. Now `toBe(4)`.
- The stub keyed its graph by bare ids while the seam receives qualified ones, so fixtures silently
  collected zero nodes. The stub now qualifies its own key space, making the mismatch structurally
  impossible rather than a per-fixture reminder.
- An invented `{nodes}` key for the neighbour response passed every arm, because the stub agreed with
  itself. The stub now answers the documented `{neighbors: [{id}]}`.

The generalisable instrument: **a stubbed seam proves the walk, never the wire shape** — and an
assertion on an *empty* result proves nothing unless you can say which mechanism produced the
emptiness.
