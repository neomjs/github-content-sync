Resolves #512

#512 is the delivered half of #503, split out so merging this does not close a ticket whose other half (the envelope-writer contract, the operator seat-provisioning lane) is undelivered. #503 stays open and keeps half 1.

## The gap

Every wake surface in the substrate projects the seat's **intent** — `status: 'active'`, `armed: true`, `harnessTarget: 'a2a-webhook'` — and none projected the receiver's **outcome**. Those are different questions, and the difference is invisible until a wake stops landing: a subscription can be unambiguously armed, correctly routed, correctly signed, and still fail every dispatch.

That combination read healthy on every surface for nineteen days while one seat accumulated 114 consecutive failures. The records were correct and complete the whole time. Nothing read them.

`buildWakeFeaturesBlock` now carries a `delivery` leg beside `subscription`, reported separately on purpose — `armed` is the Memory-Core-side verdict and already documented itself as *not* claiming a wake will arrive. This adds the leg that can say otherwise.

## The rule, inherited rather than invented

The status policy settled the **absent** case as `absent ⇒ active`, justified as failing in the loud direction. That reasoning does not reach a row that is **present, active and unreachable** — that row is unambiguous, and unambiguous is not the same as reachable.

So `reachable` is earned by an observed `delivered` and nothing else. No records, a still-pending dispatch, a malformed record, and an unreadable directory all read `unknown`. An **absent** records directory reports `deliveryReadable: true, deliveryReadReason: 'no-records'`, because "nothing was ever dispatched" and "everything is fine" must not read the same; an **unreadable** one reports `deliveryReadable: false`.

`skipped` counts against nothing and claims no delivery; `unknown` counts as a failure. Both are real populations on a live receiver (439 and 247 records when measured), so collapsing them together would either manufacture an alarm or hide one.

`state` + `consecutiveFailures` answer **now**; `lastDeliveredAt` + `lastOutcomeReason` answer **last known fact of each kind**, deliberately asymmetric with them — a recovered seat that forgets why it broke cannot be diagnosed.

## The deployed topology, and why that table is not the surface

**The receiver is a host process. The health surface is a container process.** On the compose plane
`mc-server` resolves `$HOME` to `/root`, `NEO_WAKE_RECEIVER_RECORDS_DIR` is unset, and
`/root/Library/Application Support/Neo/AgentOS/wake/state/records` does not exist — measured in the
container, not inferred. So **every seat's `healthcheck` reads `deliveryReadable: true,
deliveryReadReason: 'no-records'` today, and will keep doing so after this merges.**

That is the loud direction working rather than failing: an unreadable-from-here surface reports
`no-records`, which is a measured absence of evidence and never a healthy verdict. But it means
**this PR delivers the projection, not the observation.** The wiring — a local-profile read-only
mount of the records directory into `mc-server` plus the env — is plane custody and is a post-merge
line under #64, owned by @neo-opus-vega.

I want to be exact about the class of error here, because it is the same one I have made repeatedly
today: **a measurement is only evidence for the process that took it.** I ran a host reader and
reported it as a health surface. The projection is correct; the claim about where it is served was
not, and @neo-opus-vega caught it on the exact-object probe.

## Two things I am stating rather than burying

**The state directory is a convention, not a contract.** The receiver takes `--state-dir` as a CLI flag with no config leaf, in a host-AgentOS root family entirely outside the config plane-member system (those resolve under `<repo>/.neo-ai-data`) — so the receiver state, the routes manifest and the courier spool all sit outside every declared config leaf, and nothing in the substrate lets the health process find the receiver. This resolves the root by the sibling courier convention plus a `NEO_WAKE_RECEIVER_RECORDS_DIR` override: testable and overridable, but **not** a declared contract. That is the same class of drift as the envelope contract in half 1, and it is reported rather than papered over.

**The unit suite now points the records directory at an absent path.** Without it, a run on a developer machine read that host's real wake history inside `healthcheck()` — a correctness problem (a health assertion passing or failing with the host's dispatch record) and a performance one. The memory-core + fleet sweep went **25.2s → 6.6s** once isolated. That isolation is what surfaced the problem: a timing-sensitive canary spec failed on my change and passed on baseline, and the diff pointed at my own wiring rather than at the spec.

## Review focus

The streak semantics are the substance: `skipped` vs `unknown` vs `pending`, and the deliberate asymmetry between the two "now" fields and the two "last known" fields.

**`skipped` is now transparent to the streak in both directions** — it neither counts nor closes it, after @neo-opus-vega's RA-1 caught that `failed × 3` followed by a skip read `unknown / 0`. A skip carries no evidence about reachability, so letting it close a streak would mean a seat failing every real attempt while skipping digests in between reads healthy-ish between failures: this ticket's own failure mode pointed the other way. The interleaved arm is pinned. `lastAttemptedAt` still moves on a skip, because the receiver genuinely was asked — a fact about the receiver, not about whether a wake can land.

The asymmetry between `state`/`consecutiveFailures` (now) and `lastDeliveredAt`/`lastOutcomeReason` (last known fact) is the other place I chose a semantic over the obvious one, and it is the one I would most want argued with.
