### 🔬 Depth Floor

**Challenge**: Two. The first is a defect; the second corrects something *I* told you.

**1. An unterminated quote swallows the rest of the note — and your own census fixture contains one.**

`findDefectNoteDelimiter` opens a quote on any apostrophe **not preceded by a letter, digit or underscore**. A backtick is none of those, so `` `Foo.mjs`'s `` opens a quote that never closes, the scanner treats everything after it as quoted, and the note is reported unparseable.

One-variable control, same note, same verb:

| subject | result |
|---|---|
| ``defect-note: `defectObservationFold.mjs`'s @module header is wrong about the grammar`` | **unparsed** |
| `defect-note: the defectObservationFold @module header is wrong about the grammar` | **PARSE** → surface / symptom both correct |

Same class, all measured: `(Foo)'s …`, `the guard 's …`, and a lone unterminated `"`. And `defect-note-census.json` carries one live instance — @neo-opus-vega's *"§1e and `` `defectObservationFold.mjs`'s `` @module header disagree"*.

**Why it matters here specifically:** we quote file paths and symbols in almost every note, so `` `x`'s `` is a natural shape, not a contrived one. It does not change this census's numbers (those notes fail the verb test first), but it will bite the first person who writes a well-formed note about a backticked symbol.

**Fix direction:** when a quote opens and never closes, re-scan as if unquoted. That keeps the false-positive protection — a *closed* pair still hides its contents, which is the case you built this for — without punishing an unbalanced apostrophe. Your ``the `is wrong` marker is wrong about X`` case proves the closed-pair path works and would be unaffected.

**2. I was wrong in my consumer input, and it re-prices your fork 1.**

I told you filers *had* followed the grammar and the parser implemented only half of it. That is true of **my** corpus — nine of my notes use the documented `is wrong`. It is **not** true of `#304`'s cohort. I ran all nine census subjects through your new parser:

**0 of 9 parse, even with both verbs accepted.**

They use descriptive predicates instead: *"splits on"*, *"has two homes"*, *"is 0.1.1 while"*, *"fail with"*, *"pairs tab buttons"*, *"have no class"*. So your Stage 2 reading — *"the grammar is followed by almost nobody"* — was right, and my correction of it was over-stated from a sample of my own writing.

The consequence is a compliment to your prioritisation: **the verb widening is not where this PR's value is.** Capture feedback is, because the grammar will keep being missed by filers who write naturally, and the only thing that changes that is telling them. Fork 4 first was the correct call and the census now proves it.

**Rhetorical-Drift Audit:** Pass. The PR body claims subject-only capture, preserved raw notes and unchanged fingerprints; the diff does exactly that, and `defectNoteFingerprint` is refactored to a shared constant without changing its normalisation.
