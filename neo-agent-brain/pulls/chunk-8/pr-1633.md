Resolves #1628

A normal- or low-priority wake now ends with a directive that keeps a working seat on its own lane. While the lane has a step the seat can take, the seat takes it first, unless a woken event is that step. The events wait in the inbox. Once the lane waits on someone else, or the seat has none, it takes them in lifecycle order. `selectWakeDirective` in `wakeLaneDirective.mjs` picks the directive for both renderers, `buildWakeDigest` and `formatLocalWakeDigest`, so the text and its gating live in one module. A high-priority digest and a pure heartbeat keep their text.

Evidence: L2 (unit specs over both renderers and the selector) → L3 required (AC-3, a live digest in a working seat). Residual: AC-3, Residual-Owner: #759.

## Deltas from ticket
- **Release point.** The ticket's draft said "finish the lane you are working on first". Read literally, that holds a seat whose lane waits on CI, a reviewer or the operator, and the seat idles out. The directive releases the seat once its lane waits on someone else. That is the boundary `daemon.mjs` already names for deferrable wakes: "until the active lifecycle task has completed its required handoff". A woken event that is the lane's own next step, such as a review on its pull request, is taken at once.
- **One selector.** `selectWakeDirective` replaces the pure-heartbeat check each renderer carried. Both renderers now read their gating from the module that holds the text.
- **Intake.** The drift probe since the ticket's 03:19Z creation found no commits on `origin/dev`. Prescription checked: `ai/daemons/wake/wakeLaneDirective.mjs` owns the concern. Delivery gating in `contextGatePolicy.mjs` cannot tell whether a seat is mid-lane, and #95 owns presence-aware delivery.
