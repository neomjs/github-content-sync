Resolves #1665

Related: #1531 · #1658 · #1662 (this branch still carries their stacked commits; `dev` has them through #1661 and #1664)

The Fleet stops keeping every vendor build forever. After each family's acquisition pass, `retireHarnessGenerations` removes every admitted copy except the selected generation, its rollback and the ones a seat holds: every seat record's pin and every pin a lease of the family's seats declares on disk (ADR 0034 §2.5 item 5). It reads those holds on the family's admission chain, in the step that removes, so neither an admission nor a new pin lands between its check and a removal. Ownership it cannot prove keeps everything and names the seat: a pending Start, a lease that cannot be read, or one that is not the seat's own or pins no generation id. A retired copy's ledger entry stays, stamped `retiredAt`, so the build is never selected or fetched again; admitting it again clears the stamp.

Evidence: L2 (unit specs over real store copies) → L4 required for AC-6 (an FM build carrying this, its first pass on the live Antigravity store). Residual: AC-6, Residual-Owner: #1531.

## Deltas from ticket

- A family whose acquisition fails (a feed that is down) still retires: what the store holds does not depend on the feed.
- `materializeHarnessGeneration` clears `retiredAt` when it restores a retired build's copy. Without that, a build admitted again would read as retired with its copy present.
- Unknown ownership is wider than an unreadable lease: a pending Start selects its build before its record pins it, so it keeps everything until it publishes or releases (`FleetLifecycleService.spec.mjs` › "a pending Start keeps every copy, since it selects its build before its record pins it; once it publishes the pin or releases its attempt, retirement goes on"), and so does a lease that is not the seat's own or pins no generation id. `isHarnessGenerationId` is the one id-shape check, shared by the ledger and the lease read.
