Resolves #1789

🌿 A truncation can no longer refuse after it has already deleted the vectors.

`DatabaseService#truncateDatabase` now decides every refusal the graph step can make before it drops a single vector collection. The new private `#admitGraphTruncation` does three things in order:
1. runs the destructive-target assertion;
2. refuses a connection that is already in a transaction;
3. probes the identity publication fence through its own write path, inside an immediate transaction that it then rolls back.

The probe and the real fence therefore share one decision: served target, stored row shape and writability. The probe leaves nothing durable. After the drops, the graph's fence-and-delete transaction runs exactly as before.

Evidence: L2 (unit: the real `DatabaseService` in #1786's disposable-owner harness, with a recording vector proxy) → L2 required (both ACs are ordering behaviour inside one method). Residual: none.

| AC-2 | CI-covered: the same test. An accepted truncation drops `memory`, empties the graph, and leaves the fence `graph-truncate`, as before. |

## Deltas from ticket
- The ticket listed the open-transaction refusal as "on `dev` today". It arrived with #1786, together with the fence; both now run in the admission step (intake comment 6911332858).
- The fence check is the real fence call rolled back, not a separate read-only validator. That keeps one decision path, and it also covers an unwritable fence table, which a read could not see.
- The test's fence-call log now reads `graph-truncate` twice per truncation: the rolled-back probe, then the real fence.

## Test Evidence
Red-proof against the old order: the three refusal arms reject only after `memory` has dropped (`drops` reads `['memory', 'memory', 'memory']`, expected `[]`).
