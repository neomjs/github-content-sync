Resolves #597

Changed corpus files acquired new content-hashed rows while their older revisions stayed eligible for normal reads. Trusted profile ingestion now retires superseded rows for the supplied sources only after the replacement batch completes. Public pushes retain their additive behavior; failed, yielded, poisoned or invalid input retains prior rows. Tenant/repository boundaries, unchanged elements and unprofiled legacy rows are preserved.

### 🔬 Depth Floor

**Challenge (non-blocking):** `getSupersededSourceIds` is `await`ed at the `idsToReplace` computation site, which is **before** the MCP work-volume gate that then folds `idsToReplace.length` into its comparison. So on a large superseded profile corpus the paged enumeration — plus its inner per-50 metadata fetches — is incurred, and only then can the guard raise `KB_SYNC_VOLUME_EXCEEDED`. The gate's own comment says it exists because a large deletion once "returned before the gate was ever evaluated"; the new cost is now paid on the other side of that same line. I am not treating this as a Required Action because it produces no incorrect state, the paging is bounded, and the guard still fires — but if you want the cost ordering to match the guard's stated intent, computing a cheap `sourcePath` count first and gating on that would put the guard back in front of the work.

I also checked and cleared: cross-tenant blast radius (`ownedScope` is `$and`-composed, never replaced); legacy-row survival (the `[a-f0-9]{64}` `extractionIdentity` test is applied on re-read, not just on the input filter); infinite-paging risk (`batch.ids.length === limit` is evaluated on the unfiltered page, and an empty page exits); and report honesty (`deleted` is the only metric changed, and the sets are disjoint).
