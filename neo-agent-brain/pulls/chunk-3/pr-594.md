Resolves #593

🌿 A service starved at its CPU quota now reads as starved, and "healthy" is no longer what the plane's own diagnosis says about a Memory Core waiting on the scheduler in half its periods.

mc-server ran at a one-CPU quota and was throttled in 10,670 of 19,124 CPU periods (186 s) while the deployment snapshot reported `diagnosis: healthy`. ADR-0025 §2.4's detect signal, "CPU load sampled over a window", was implemented as Docker's per-core percent against a fixed `cpuSaturationPercent: 90`. That reading is blind to the quota (one busy core of four counts as saturated, and a one-CPU container at its cap reads about 100) and blind to bursts (a service at its cap every period averages under 90). The quota's own verdict, `cpu_stats.throttling_data`, arrives in every stats sample the service already collects and was never read.

- `summarizeCpuThrottling` reads the throttled share of periods across the sampled window. A counter reset, a container with no quota and a single sample are unmeasured, never zero.
- A `cpu-throttling` fact at `cpuThrottledRatio` (0.25) over the CPU clock's window, non-authoritative like restart churn. The classifier's last branch records it (`ambiguous` + `record`, `cpu-throttling-recorded`). A restart adds no CPU, and no lifecycle action admits a CPU raise, so it records and licenses nothing.
- `resolveCpuQuota`: CPU percents are read against `HostConfig.NanoCpus` where set; the saturation fact names the `quotaCpus` it used. An unset quota keeps the per-core reading.

Evidence: L2 (unit: the helpers, the mc-server shape recorded and never restarted, below-ratio and short-window absence, an actionable route kept, quota-relative saturation; mutation-killed) → L4 for AC-4 (the live plane's mc-server row carries the fact). Residual: AC-4, Residual-Owner: #64 (where it tests the AC-9 / AC-10 hypothesis).

Not micro-review eligible: a new detect fact and classifier branch in ADR-0025's immune system.
