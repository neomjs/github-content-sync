## RA-1 (required, pre-merge) — the JSDoc claims an equivalence the park admission does not have

The method JSDoc says:

> a child that publishes no outer rect samples its inner one, **as the park admission does**

**It does not.** `NativeVesselTransaction.mjs:366-367` reads both rects on the descriptor's single `plane`:

```js
sourceRect = sourceWindow?.[plane] ?? null,
targetRect = targetWindow?.[plane] ?? null,
```

There is no per-child fallback. `rectPlane` is a **caller-level choice**, and its own JSDoc (`:313-316`) frames staying on inner as a deliberate decision by a consumer whose admission does not depend on the frame — not as a degradation when the requested plane yields nothing. So the admission is **fail-closed** (`?? null`, which then refuses at `:394`) and this change is **fail-open**.

I think the open behaviour is the better one, and I would argue for keeping it: refusing an otherwise-authorized live vessel over a missing frame is exactly what that `rectPlane` JSDoc warns against, and a child that publishes no `outerRect` would otherwise never convert. **But that is a divergence from the authority being invoked, so it has to be declared as one rather than asserted as a match.** A future maintainer who reads "as the park admission does" and then goes looking for the fallback will not find it, and will draw a conclusion about the admission that is wrong.

Suggested replacement for that clause:

> a child that publishes no outer rect samples its inner one. This DIVERGES from the park admission, which is fail-closed on its single declared plane (`sourceWindow?.[plane] ?? null`, refusing at `NativeVesselTransaction:394`). Failing open is deliberate here: the alternative refuses an otherwise-authorized live vessel over a missing frame, which is the outcome `rectPlane`'s own JSDoc warns against.

## Non-blocking, but owed before merge: the ADR, and a claim that outruns what is established

**The coherence argument is the right one and it is not yet complete.** The PR's rationale — "the conversion that proposes the park now measures the same two planes the park admission reads" — does not hold on the target side, and this is worth knowing precisely because the whole justification is coherence.

- ADR 0029 §2.8.6, verbatim: *"Its admission reads the source's live **outer** extent and the target's live **inner** extent."*
- The implementation, `:366-367`: **both** rects on the **same** `plane`, and `VesselWorkspace.mjs:333` passes `rectPlane: 'outer'` — so the admission reads the target's **outer** extent.
- This change moves the metric's **source** to outer. The metric's **target** plane is unchanged.

So source now agrees and **target does not**: the metric compares source-outer against the target plane the projection supplies, while the admission compares outer against outer. That is a pre-existing ADR-vs-code divergence on the target side, not something this PR introduced — but this PR is the first to make it load-bearing, because it is the first to claim the two surfaces agree.

Two honest ways to land, and the choice is yours:

1. **The ADR is stale about the target.** Amend §2.8.6 to say the admission reads both on one plane, and note that the metric now matches it. Coherence achieved, and the dangling reference goes with it.
2. **The admission is wrong about the target.** Then the metric's current target plane is right, the admission needs its own fix, and §2.8.6 stands.

I am not picking. Note also that this is the same defect I flagged in the ticket's V-B-A and it is now **two** problems, not one: §2.8.4's min-axis metric is cited once at `:618` and defined nowhere, **and** §2.8.6's admission sentence is not what `:366-367` implements. The ticket's own step — "amend ADR 0029 §2.8.4/§2.8.6 to give the metric a normative home, so this fork cannot reopen" — is owed either way, and I will take it if you would rather not.
