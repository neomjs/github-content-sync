# PR Micro-Review

**Class:** contained. The gesture drivers' arming predicate gains two terms (`!dragStartPending`, `dragProxy.mounted`). A hand-rolled poll becomes the existing `waitFor`, and the birth-failure diagnostic gains `proxyMounted`. Four files, none on the VDom update path (`src/` is untouched), so it carries no engine concept.

**Verdict:** Approved

**Glance:** The claim is that the hosted `exitFired=false` failures come from the driver flinging outward before the drag proxy mounts, so every outward sample crosses Main with `proxyRect: null`. I checked it three ways:
- **Your receipt on #22114 (7158584055):** red on `dev` `73e6207999` on Trial 1 under 135/140 ms CPU preemption of the VDom SharedWorker. The read-only sample shows `{"proxyMounted":false,"dragStartPending":true}`, and the run went 5/5 after the fix. That reproduces the hosted signature, where page-level throttling could not.
- **`dev` source:** both new terms are real state. `DragZone#dragStartPending` is set at `:183` and cleared at `:191` around the async start, and `dragProxy.mounted` is the proxy component's mount flag.
- **CI at `8451a94ac3`:** `unit`, `components` and `e2e-engine` (all 7 selected shards) are green. The new unit arms pin each term in isolation, for both `GestureDriver` and `DemoBTearOutGesture`.

The prior-PR sweep (`gh pr list --state all --search 22114`) shows no earlier attempt at this ticket.

What I looked for: whether gating the driver hides a product race. A human pointer keeps sampling after the proxy mounts, so a later sample still crosses the boundary with a `proxyRect`. Waiting in the driver therefore models a real pointer and does not paper over the engine. One case is not covered: a fling that exits and stops before the proxy mounts sends no later sample. That is a product hypothesis outside #22114, not a finding here. The `NativeGestureDriver` stack-return gate has no unit arm of its own; `WorkstationStackReturnSplitNL` covers it in the e2e tier.
