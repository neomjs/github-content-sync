Resolves #21620

🌿 A planner in Tokyo no longer has to ask what the Berlin rota means on their own clock.

Related: #21346 · #21140 · #21613

Adds `examples/scheduler/timeZones`: a follow-the-sun support rota for the weekend of 31 October, nine agents in Tokyo, Berlin and New York, with every shift stored in UTC. A segmented switch sets the time axis to the viewer's own zone, Berlin, New York or Tokyo. The zone and the range go into one `timeAxis.set()`, so the range's day keys resolve on the new clock: the hours and day boundaries keep their place on screen, and every shift moves to its time in that city. Each day's header names the city and its offset. On Sunday 1 November New York leaves daylight time, so in New York time that day has 25 hours, `01:00` comes twice, and the header reads `UTC−4 → UTC−5`. Each bar names its shift on the agent's own clock, and a line under the toolbar shows the chosen shift's stored UTC times beside the chosen clock. No engine change, no shared file.

Evidence: L3 (Chromium at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844, five themes, the browser pinned to Los Angeles; `TimeZones.spec.mjs` in `e2e-engine`; the frames and cold read below) → L3 required (AC-1 to AC-7 are rendered layout, zone switching and look). Residual: none for #21620; publication belongs to #21613.

## Deltas from ticket

- The weekend of 31 October 2026 puts a clock change inside the plan: New York's on Sunday at 02:00, and Los Angeles's too for a West Coast viewer. Every agent works the same local hours on both days, so New York's Sunday shifts are stored one hour later than its Saturday ones. The axis spans Friday to Monday in every zone, so no shift falls off it in any city.
- Switching the zone re-anchors the axis on Friday midnight of the chosen city. The day boundaries keep their place on screen, and the shifts move past them. That is the ticket's "every shift moves and the day boundary moves with it", read from the shifts.
- Each bar names its shift on its agent's own clock (`Sat 07:00 – 15:00 Tokyo time`), so the axis and the label read two clocks side by side. A click or Enter on a bar chooses the shift for the line under the toolbar, and the timeline column's `highlightedRecordIds` marks it.
- Below 720px the zone switch is a two-by-two grid, and the plan opens at the chosen shift and follows it across zones; at full width it opens at Friday 20:00 and keeps the clock time in view, so the shifts visibly move.
- The drive found no engine defect that blocks this ticket. It found one behaviour worth a look, filed as a defect-note: `timeAxis.set({timeZone})` alone keeps bare-key `start` and `end` as instants, so day ticks fall at another hour in the new zone, while bare `nonWorkingDates` keep their civil dates. The example passes the range in the same `set()`, which is correct usage today.
