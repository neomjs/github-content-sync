Resolves #21006

## What changes

One spec, `test/playwright/e2e/core/ContentSecurityPolicy.spec.mjs`, boots an example under one named policy and asserts that nothing in the policy was violated:

```
default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; worker-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; connect-src 'self'
```

The header is stamped on the document **and on every script response**, because a worker takes its policy from its own script response rather than from the page that started it. The signal is the `securitypolicyviolation` event, not console text — the console API never sees a violation raised inside a worker.

No directive is widened beyond the starting policy: the example boots clean, and the test is the measurement.

## The example is `examples/grid/container`, not `examples/grid/bigData`

The ticket proposed `bigData`. Its `neo-config.json` sets `"useAiClient": true`, which loads `src/ai/client/RuntimeService.mjs` — one of the two modules that calls `new Function`, needing `'unsafe-eval'` by design. So it is exactly the app this policy cannot certify, and the suggestion contradicts the AC's own rationale. `examples/grid/container` loads neither `LivePreview` nor the Neural Link client.

## The witness is proven, not assumed (AC-3)

Two arms, and the second is why a green here means anything:

| Arm | Policy | Result |
|---|---|---|
| certification | the policy above | 2 workers' scripts stamped, 0 violations, 0 console errors, grid rendered |
| control | `default-src 'self'; script-src 'none'` | violations recorded with a `script-src*` directive, app never renders |

A recorded run with the **certification arm red**, by putting `script-src 'none'` into the policy under test:

```
x  1 › Content security policy › the engine boots under the named policy without a violation (30.3s)
    TimeoutError: page.waitForFunction: Timeout 30000ms exceeded.
  ok 2 › Content security policy › the witness reports a violation when the policy denies the boot
  1 failed
  1 passed (36.2s)
```

That is the failure the spec reports when the policy denies the boot; with the policy restored:

```
ok 1 › the engine boots under the named policy without a violation (2.5s)
ok 2 › the witness reports a violation when the policy denies the boot (234ms)
2 passed (8.7s)
```

## AC-2 is an intersection, not a hope

`page.on('worker')` gives the URLs of the workers the page actually started; the route records which script responses carried the header. The assertion is that at least one observed worker URL appears among the stamped script paths — so a future Playwright that stops intercepting worker fetches turns this red instead of silently measuring the document only.

## AC-5, the page

`learn/benefits/EnterpriseEvaluation.md`: the sentence "The engine has not been certified against a named content security policy" now names the policy, the example and the spec, says the witness proves itself, and claims **one example under the default configuration** — not a certification of the reader's application, and not the portal or any deployed site. The matching bullet in "What Is Not Here Yet" goes.

## Out of scope, as the ticket says

`LivePreview` and the Neural Link client under a policy, Trusted Types, a policy for the portal. No violation was found in the engine, so there is no linked ticket to open for one.

## One thing found on the way, not fixed here

`buildScripts/util/developmentThemeAssets.mjs#runDevelopmentThemeBuild` spawns `npm.cmd` without `shell: true`. On Windows with Node ≥ 20 that is `spawn EINVAL`, so the E2E `globalSetup` cannot run its theme preflight and the whole tier refuses to start for a local contributor; building the themes by hand first works around it because the preflight then returns early. Linux CI never reaches it. Say the word and I will open a ticket or send the one-line fix separately.
