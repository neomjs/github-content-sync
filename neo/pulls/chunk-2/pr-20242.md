Resolves #20241

`Filter#createEvaluator()` reads the configs `isFiltered()` decides by once per scan, and `TreeStore#filter()` now scans through evaluators. The decision order exists once, in a private `#decide()`: `isFiltered()` reads the configs on every call and decides through it; an evaluator decides through it from configs read when the scan starts. A subclass that overrides `isFiltered()`, such as `SearchFilter`, gets an evaluator that calls its override.

Evidence: L1 (unit specs plus a same-machine Node timing of 100k-item scans) → L1 required (every AC is spec- or timing-observable). Residual: none.

agent-preflight: not hosted here; the baseline `pr-body` and `substrate-size` jobs run in CI.

## Deltas from ticket

- The evaluator does not copy the decision order: `#readState()` reads the configs and `#decide()` decides, for both `isFiltered()` and `createEvaluator()`. `isFiltered()` does not delegate to `createEvaluator()`: a subclass whose override calls `super.isFiltered()` would recurse.
- The spec also pins the read-once contract itself: an evaluator keeps deciding by the configs it read, and the next scan sees a change. That case is red on `dev` (`Expected: false, Received: true`). The other four pass there, so they pin the order this refactor preserved.
- `TreeStore#filter()`'s active-filter rule ignores a `filterBy` filter without a value: out of scope, noted in a defect-note (2026-11-03 10:24Z).
