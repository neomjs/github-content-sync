# dev merges through a merge queue that retests the PRs behind each merge

## Context

The operator, 19 Nov: "maybe even CI restarts on its own as soon as a PR gets merged that can affect others." Tonight five approved examples PRs went stale after each merge into `dev`; their authors merged `dev` in by hand, one by one, and each push re-ran the full CI. Nothing re-tests open PRs when `dev` moves: Review Admission Mergeability runs one job of 12–15 s per push to `dev`, and `same-head-check-repair.yml` re-runs earlier red runs of the same head only.

## The Problem

Staleness is handled by people. Every merge into `dev` turns approved PRs into rebase work for their authors (Brain #1869 counts the wakes), and the PR that merges next waits for its author to notice, merge, push and pass CI again.

## The Architectural Reality

Design authority: n/a. This adds a merge path; the merge itself stays the operator's (critical gate 1).

- GitHub's merge queue builds a temporary branch per queued PR on top of `dev` plus the PRs ahead of it, runs the required checks there, and merges in order. It squashes as today; textual conflicts still leave the queue.
- Its checks run on the `merge_group` event. The workflows `dev`'s merges depend on trigger on `pull_request` or `pull_request_target` today: `test-e2e.yml`, `test.yml`, `build.yml`, `pr-baseline.yml`, `review-admission-mergeability.yml`, `file-size-guard.yml`, `codeql-analysis.yml`.
- Requiring the queue on `dev` is a branch-protection setting, which is the operator's. Its availability on this organisation's plan is the first thing to confirm.

## The Fix

1. The workflows that gate a merge also run on `merge_group`, with test-scope classification and sharding working on a merge-group ref.
2. The operator enables the queue on `dev` and names its required checks.
3. The Fleet treats a queued PR as held: no `rebase` wake while it waits (Brain #1869, option 2).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] AC-1: The required workflows run and pass on a `merge_group` event: a trial queue on a scratch branch, or the first queued PR.
- [ ] AC-2: Two queued PRs that don't conflict textually both merge without either author pushing after approval.
- [ ] AC-3: A queued PR that conflicts textually leaves the queue and its author gets one wake.

## Out of Scope

- Splitting the shared catalog files (#21539), which removes the textual conflicts the queue cannot.
- Runner capacity (filed alongside).
