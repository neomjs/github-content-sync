# Scheduler, Gantt and Task Board show ten examples each

Terminal predicate: a first-time visitor opens the Scheduler, the Gantt or the Task Board group of the Portal's examples and finds ten demonstrations of that family, each answering one question a buyer of a planning component asks and each opening on a frame worth a cover; and every open leaf under this epic can be finished by a contributor from its own body.

## Problem scope

**The operator, 18 Nov 2026:** "task board, gantt charts, scheduler => gut feeling need 10+ examples each … it really relates to neo's inventory." On the structure, the same day: one epic for the three families, not one each, and tickets for the community "only if the scope is rock-solid".

**What exists.** One example per family: `examples/scheduler/basic`, `examples/gantt/basic`, `examples/board/basic`, built with the families themselves (#20874). A buyer who compares the engine with a commercial scheduler or Gantt chart finds one frame per family.

**What the engine carries and no example shows** (config names on `dev` `ba9e742567`; names, not yet behaviour):

- `src/scheduler`, `src/timeline`: `barDraggable`, `resizableBars`, `editable` with an event editor, `minimumDuration`, `barRenderer`, `percentDoneField`, `resourceColumns`, `showNonWorkingTime`; on the time axis `nonWorkingDays`, `nonWorkingDates`, `snapMinutes`, `timeZone` and the tick units; bars clipped to the viewport over buffered rows.
- `src/gantt`: `cascadeDependencies`, `dependencyCreation`, `useWorkingTime`, `highlightedDependencyIds`, a task tree with one task-edit writer.
- `src/board`: `lanes` and `laneField` (swimlanes), `wipLimit`, `locked` and `collapsed` columns, `maxRenderedCards` with `overscan` (a windowed column), `columnsResortable`, card item renderers, an inline title editor and a card editor, a selection model with keyboard navigation, `enableProxyToPopup` on the drag zones.

**Read against the source on 18 Nov 2026** ([Grace's check](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/21346#issuecomment-6982869553)): 13 candidates are carried, 13 in part, 1 is missing. The corrected wording stands in the three rows of #21140's content table, with the engine gaps behind it. Nothing is driven yet.

**Why an epic, and why one.** About 27 one-example leaves that share their rules: what makes a planning example, how its look is read, how its scope is made safe for a contributor. One body holds those rules once. Three epics would repeat them and stand nearly empty until the candidates are checked. This epic splits per family when it passes about 25 leaves.

## Intended solution shape

1. **The candidates are the rows Scheduler, Gantt and Task Board of the content table in #21140's body:** nine per family, read off the config names above. A candidate is a lead.
2. **A leaf exists only for a checked candidate.** Checked means: the capability was driven on `dev` in the family's base example, and it works. A candidate that needs engine work becomes an engine ticket and waits there; it gets no example leaf. The first check is investigation 2 of #21140's roadmap. The cut's three are the exception: they are filed on the source check and built by maintainers, and the drive is each leaf's first step (#21355, #21356, #21357).
3. **What makes a leaf rock-solid.** Every leaf fills the same frame, so that a contributor with a coding agent can finish it from public context alone:
   - the visitor's question, in one sentence;
   - the config or class that answers it, with file and line;
   - the folder and the record's id, title and caption;
   - the data: what the records are, how many, where they come from (shipped with the example, never a server);
   - the first frame at 1440×900 and at 390×844 in words: what is on screen at rest, and what the one named action shows;
   - the checks: no console error, the phone size, the keyboard where the capability has one, both of the example's themes;
   - the look: before and after on the pull request, read cold by the reviewer's seat.
   A leaf gets `good first issue` and `help wanted` only when every line of that frame is filled.
4. **One example is one folder, one record, one cover and one pull request,** as under #21175. A base example is the template; a new example does not edit it.
5. **Order.** For the cut of Mon 23 Nov: a year for 300 resources (#21355), 2,000 tasks (#21356) and swimlanes (#21357), built by maintainers. After the cut, in the order of a buyer's questions: does it scale, can people edit it by drag and in an editor, does it know working time, can it zoom, can a bar or a card be designed, does it update itself. They land week by week; the cut waits for none of them.
6. **Ten is the depth the operator named, not a quota.** A family that answers eight questions well does not get two fillers.

## Out of scope

- The engine families and their theme files (#20874, #20879), and any capability the engine does not have yet: that is an engine ticket.
- A capability of a commercial package (the rule of #20498, until 1 Dec). Every candidate is a config of the public engine under `src/`.
- The renewal of the three base examples: rows of #21140, done with their group's catalog pass.
- Calendar depth: its event model has no recurrence and no resources (investigation 4 of #21140's roadmap).
- Loading from a server for these families: deferred in `ROADMAP.md`.

## Avoided traps

- **One epic per family today.** Three bodies with the same rules, each nearly empty until the check.
- **A leaf from a config name.** A contributor builds on a capability that turns out to need engine work, and the pull request cannot merge.
- **A curated label on a leaf with a blank in its frame.** The blank is where a contributor guesses.
- **A scale example that fetches.** Its data is generated in the example, with its count on screen.
