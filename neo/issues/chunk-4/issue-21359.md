# The functional button and nested-components examples read as examples

Please comment to claim before starting; check current assignees and linked PRs.

## Context

The Functional Components group of the Portal's examples shows its entries as tiles on the examples workbench (#21140; the group's rows are in [the slate](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/21142#issuecomment-6980292611)). Two of its five entries still carry what their authors needed while building them. A leaf of #21358.

## The Problem

Opened on `dev` `0128668721` at 1440×900 and 390×844, and re-read in source on `dev` `289d1a2db3`:

- **`examples/functional/button/base`**: the example button sits 500 px below the top of an empty stage, with 1,500 px of empty page under it. `MainContainer.mjs:191` gives it `style: {marginBottom: '1500px', marginTop: '500px'}`, a scroll test. A click shows nothing: line 188 sets `handler: data => console.log('button click =>', data.component.id)`.
- **`examples/functional/nestedComponents`**: the headings count 1, 2, 3, 2 in the order a reader meets them: "1 · Functional root" (`Component.mjs:36`), "2 · Classic container" (`Component.mjs:38`), "3 · Functional child through the classic container" (`RelayContainer.mjs:24`), "2 · Direct functional child" (`Component.mjs:40`). The intro (`Component.mjs:35`) says "Change the root values in Configuration", and the panel it means is labelled "Inspector" (`examples/ConfigurationViewport.mjs:489`).

## The Architectural Reality

- Both entries are workbench pages: their `MainContainer` extends `Neo.examples.ConfigurationViewport` (`examples/ConfigurationViewport.mjs`), which shows the component returned by `createExampleComponent()` beside an Inspector for its configs. The Inspector's label is shared by every workbench entry.
- The button is `Neo.functional.button.Base` (`src/functional/button/Base.mjs`), which has `badgeText`, `text` and `iconCls` configs among others.
- `test/playwright/e2e/rendering/NestedComponentsNL.spec.mjs` loads the nested-components folder. It asserts values and click counts (lines 43 to 54), not the headings or the intro.
- Neither entry has a record in `apps/portal/resources/data/examples.json`. Their tiles come from the recipe and the tile list of #21332 and #21333.
- To look at an entry: `npm run server-start`, then open `/examples/functional/button/base/` and `/examples/functional/nestedComponents/`.

## The Fix

1. **Button:** remove the two margins. Give a click a result a visitor can see in the page, inside the example file: for instance the badge counts the clicks. No `console` call remains in the folder.
2. **Nested components:** number the four headings in the order they appear, or in a scheme that follows the nesting (for instance 1, 2, 2.1, 3). Say in the pull request which one and why. The intro names the Inspector.
3. Both entries read well at 1440×900 and at 390×844, in the light and in the dark theme.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] `examples/functional/button/base`: the button is in view at rest at both sizes, a click changes something visible in the page, and the folder holds no `console.` call.
- [ ] `examples/functional/nestedComponents`: the headings number in a consistent order, and the intro names the Inspector.
- [ ] `test/playwright/e2e/rendering/NestedComponentsNL.spec.mjs` passes; a change to it is explained in the pull request.
- [ ] The pull request shows both entries before and after at 1440×900 and 390×844.
- [ ] No file outside the two folders and that spec changes; no record and no cover is added.

## Out of Scope

- The tile recipe and the tile list (#21332, #21333).
- `examples/functional/templateComponent`, which gets no tile, and the three functional entries that already read well (`defineComponent`, `hostComponent`, `nestedTemplateComponent`).
- A new functional example.

## Avoided Traps

- **A toast or a dialog to show the click.** The entry shows a button. A config of the button itself changing is the smallest result a visitor can see, and it adds no second component to explain.
- **Renaming the Inspector to match the intro.** The label belongs to every workbench entry; the one sentence is what is wrong.
