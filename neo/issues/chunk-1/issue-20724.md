# Make example cards lighter without hiding their screenshots

## Context

The operator requested a design follow-up after #20703 merged: “black cards don't exactly look nice,” and suggested dark blue / purple surfaces with some transparency. This leaf belongs to #20498's examples overview outcome.

The existing cover ticket #20679 explicitly excludes caption copy and the caption scrim. It does not cover this work. The functional catalog/navigation leaves #20500 and #20690 remain complete.

## The Problem

The image-card caption uses `background-color: rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.88)` in `resources/scss/src/apps/portal/examples/List.scss:263-276`. That preserves text legibility but creates a heavy inset black rectangle. Long captions also cover much of the screenshot on a phone. Changing only the hue improves the tone while retaining that composition problem.

The default cover gradient is grey, so cards without a screenshot need to belong to the selected visual system too. The comparison must preserve the demonstration's visible detail and the feature / observation / limitation copy.

Design authority: the operator's 2026-11-10 request to reconsider this card treatment and explore blue/purple semitransparency.

## The Architectural Reality

- `apps/portal/view/examples/List.mjs#createItemContent` owns the cover, placeholder and caption composition; the canonical Model / Store records remain the content authority.
- `resources/scss/src/apps/portal/examples/List.scss` owns geometry, the current black surface, the cover blend/gradient and fallback treatment.
- The nearby Connect view separates structural glass rules from dark/light palette custom properties in `resources/scss/{src,theme-neo-dark,theme-neo-light}/apps/portal/services/Container.scss`. Use that ownership pattern with Examples-specific aliases of existing Portal/semantic theme tokens, not independent card-only colours or Services variable names.
- `ExamplesCatalogNL.spec.mjs:112-119` currently tests caption background alpha >= 0.85 and padding >= 12. It does not require black, but this is an implementation proxy: a translucent gradient can have a transparent computed `background-color` while painting an adequate surface. Update the oracle to the chosen treatment.

Prescription checked: this belongs in the existing Examples List and its SCSS/theme owners. No engine primitive, metadata sidecar or new design subsystem is needed.

## Options and Current Preference

| Direction | Treatment | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Tinted glass** — operator's preferred candidate | Use a full-width navy → violet glass sheet docked to the bottom edge on wide cards, with a bounded height and restrained local blur; put the caption below the image on phones. Derive production colours from existing Portal theme tokens. | Preserves the preferred surface while removing the busy inset frame. Keep at least half the cover uncovered with the longest caption; a readable no-blur fallback is required. |
| **Preview + caption** | Give the screenshot its own visible upper region, with copy in a blue/violet surface below. Keep copy in normal flow so long mobile captions can grow. | Addresses both the colour and image-occlusion problems. Cards may become taller; compare density and crop before choosing. |
| **Full-bleed gradient** | Remove the inset box on wide cards; strengthen a continuous theme-derived blue/violet scrim toward the bottom. Put the caption below the image on phones. | More cohesive than a black rectangle, but busy images and long captions still need a strong lower wash. |

Steward design input (Clio, 2026-11-10): captions must not cover the screenshot at phone width; the separated composition must be compared at 375px regardless of the desktop colour choice. Production tint derives from existing Portal theme tokens in both appearances. Put captures of the three candidates at 375px and 1440px, light and dark, on this ticket for her design read before the operator pins a direction. Conversation mockups are exploratory, not that acceptance receipt.

The initial exploration favoured **Preview + caption** for image visibility. After viewing the comparison, the operator prefers **Tinted glass**: it looks nicer to him than the separated composition. Clio's [provisional design read](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/20724#issuecomment-6769335528) agrees that glass is the better surface and refines the candidate into a full-width, bottom-docked sheet rather than an inset panel. The actual-Portal comparisons and final production pin remain outstanding; keep the phone-separation and theme-token constraints above.

### Refined glass candidate

- Dock the sheet to the bottom edge at full width; remove the inset frame of cropped screenshot fragments.
- At 1440px, keep at least half the cover uncovered with the longest catalog caption. Let the card grow instead of letting the sheet climb or truncating essential copy. If that cannot fit, use the separated composition on that evidence.
- At 375px, the caption is below the image in both main candidates; check that glass without an image behind it still belongs to the same visual family.
- Read the weakest point of the light-theme composite as well as the dark one. A light frosted sheet with dark text may be the appropriate theme expression; avoid simply turning the black rectangle blue.
- Tint must carry legibility without backdrop blur. Keep blur restrained and scroll the full catalog with it enabled before accepting the treatment.

Apply transparency to the surface background, never to the whole card, text or icons. Do not blur the demonstration itself. Coordinate any changed image frame/crop with #20679's cover composition; do not absorb its capture work.

## Out of Scope

## Avoided Traps

- Lowering the whole card's opacity: it fades the text as well as the surface.
- Recolouring the rectangle while ignoring how much demonstration it obscures.
- A literal colour/alpha assertion promoted to a readability guarantee.
- Truncating the feature/observation copy or making it hover-only to fit a fixed height.
- Adding one-off npm-ignore entries for design evidence.

### @neo-fable-clio - 2026-11-10T14:02:41Z

**Design read of the two sketches the operator compared** (`Tinted glass` and `Preview + caption`, the Issues cover, dark). Provisional: the pin waits for the Portal captures at 375 and 1440, light and dark.

**Tinted glass is the better surface. As drawn it does not yet pass the one requirement it has to pass.**

1. **Agreed with the operator on the surface.** The navy-to-violet glass belongs to the dark theme, it has depth, and the demonstration stays present behind it. The separated variant reads as a coloured box under a thumbnail, and its screenshot becomes texture at that size.
2. **The visibility problem is the panel's height and its inset.** In the sketch the panel covers about 60 % of the card's height. What stays clear is the top third of the cover: the search field, the header row and two rows, the least telling part of a grid. Around the inset panel a frame of cut-off words remains (right edge and bottom), and that frame is the busiest thing on the card.
3. **So: glass as a sheet docked to the bottom edge, full width, with a height cap.** No inset frame, and the cover's upper half stays whole. With the longest caption in the catalog the card grows taller; the sheet does not climb. Measured on the 1440 capture: at least half of the cover uncovered for that longest caption. If the cap cannot hold it, the copy on the card face is too long for any overlay, and `Preview + caption` wins on that evidence.
4. **At 375 the two candidates are the same card.** The caption sits below the image in both, so the phone capture decides nothing between them. It only has to show that the tinted surface without an image behind it still looks like the same family.
5. **Light theme is the open risk.** A navy sheet over a light screenshot can be the black rectangle again, in blue. The light capture may need a light frosted sheet with dark text from the same tokens. Check the body copy's contrast on the composite at its weakest point, not on the declared colour.
6. **Blur is garnish.** The tint carries legibility; the sheet must read without `backdrop-filter`. Keep the blur small and scroll the full catalog once with it on: this page should not be where the Portal drops frames.

**My preference between the two as drawn:** the separated composition serves a demonstration better, the glass looks better. The docked sheet is both, which is why I would put it on the ticket as the tinted-glass candidate instead of the inset panel. Which one looks nicer in the end is the operator's eye.
