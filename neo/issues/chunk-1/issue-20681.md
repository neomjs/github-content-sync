# Row geometry: an extra height per row that the estimate ignores

## Context

In content-sized mode, `Neo.grid.RowGeometry` holds one height per record and estimates every unmeasured row from the mean of the measured ones. That mean works for rows sized by their cells. It goes wrong as soon as a row grows by something that is not a cell: a detail panel under an expanded row, an inline form, a notes strip. That extra height enters the same mean.

## The Problem

`RowGeometry` stores one height per record (`setHeights`, `src/grid/RowGeometry.mjs:334`), and `measuredMean` averages all of them (`:117`). After each measure batch, `grid.View` re-derives the estimate for unmeasured rows from that mean: `View#runAheadMeasure` calls `setRowHeights(heights, true)` (`src/grid/View.mjs:1264`). A batch covers the visible rows plus eight beyond each overscan edge.

So a consumer that grows a row by an extra part has to pass the composite height, and the composite height moves the estimate. With illustrative numbers: 50 measured rows of 32px, one of them expanded by a 300px panel, give a mean of 38px. Every unmeasured row of a 100k-row grid is then estimated about 19% too tall, and the scroll extent shrinks back as rows get measured. Allowing only one expanded row bounds the error to `extra / measured rows`, which is largest exactly when the first screen is measured.

A font refresh also drops the extra: `View#commitFontHeights` calls `rowGeometry.clearHeights()` and re-applies the measured text heights only (`View.mjs:806–815`).

## The Architectural Reality

- `RowGeometry` owns the row heights, the offsets and the estimate of one view. `grid.View` is its only writer, through the scroll-stable `#changeRowHeights` path (`setRowHeights`, `resetRowHeights`, `commitFontHeights`).
- `measuredMean`'s JSDoc: "Mean height of measured records, or null before the first measurement. Unseen rows do not count".
- Design authority: n/a. This adds a capability, and the estimate keeps its meaning: the mean of measured heights.
- Precedent: the content-sized rows work in #19668, #19674 and #19688.

## The Fix

- **`RowGeometry#setExtraHeights(heights)`**: a `Map<recordId, Number>` of extra px per record, kept beside the measured heights.
  - A row's height is its measured height, or the estimate, plus its extra. Offsets and `totalHeight` include the extra.
  - `0` or `null` removes an entry.
  - `measuredMean` stays the mean of the measured heights only.
  - `clearHeights()` keeps the extras, because a font refresh re-measures text, not panels. A new store starts without any, as it does for measured heights.
- **`grid.View#setRowExtraHeights(heights)`** applies them through `#changeRowHeights`, so the row at the scroll offset holds still. During a thumb drag it waits for the drag's end, as `setRowHeights` does.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] `measuredMean` ignores extras: measured heights of `32` and `32`, plus an extra of `300` on one of the records, keep the mean at `32` (unit).
- [ ] Offsets and `totalHeight` include extras, and removing an extra restores them (unit).
- [ ] `clearHeights()` keeps extras (unit).
- [ ] When an extra above the row at the scroll offset changes, `View#setRowExtraHeights` keeps that row still on screen (unit or e2e, following the existing content-sized specs).
- [ ] Fixed-height grids are unaffected: they have no `RowGeometry`, and `setRowExtraHeights` returns `false`.

## Out of Scope

- Any component that renders the extra part, such as a detail panel or an editor.
- Which rows may grow, and whether they shrink when scrolled away. That stays the consumer's UX choice, and this ticket makes it independent of the estimate.
