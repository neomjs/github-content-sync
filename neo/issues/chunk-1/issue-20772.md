# GraphScene orbits on a drag that began outside its canvas

## Context

Design authority: two summaries in the engine. `Neo.canvas.GraphScene` says "a primary-button drag orbits" (`src/canvas/GraphScene.mjs:83`), and `Neo.canvas.Base#onMouseDown` says "a button went down on the canvas … so a drag gesture starts here" (`src/canvas/Base.mjs:187`). Both describe a gesture that starts with a press on the canvas.

Observed on 2026-11-11 in the design read of neomjs/neo-agent-institution#911. The Institution's visual arm `the side panel is as wide as the operator drags it` widens the Observatory's side panel with a real mouse drag on a splitter that sits beside the canvas. Its re-captured golden showed the graph rotated and one well name gone.

Witness (Institution `dev` `6f00d3d`, engine `0baab546`, Chromium, the arm's own gesture: press on the splitter, 200 px left in 8 steps, release):

- 6 of the 8 button-held `mousemove` events target the canvas, with a `movementX` sum of −150 px. The panel resizes one round trip after the pointer moves, so the pointer runs ahead of the splitter onto the canvas. At `orbitSpeed` 0.006 that is 0.9 rad of yaw.
- The pane before the drag and the pane after dragging 200 px left and 200 px back, at the same size again, differ in 21,469 pixels, and the well name "REM digests the backlog before the next cut" is gone. The camera kept the rotation.

## The Problem

`GraphScene#updateMouseState` orbits on every pointer report that carries the primary button and a movement. It never asks whether the press happened on its own canvas. A press on a neighbouring control that is then dragged across the canvas turns the scene: a splitter, a drag-and-drop item, a text selection that runs over the canvas.

The same report sets `camera.touched` and clears `flightPlan`. After such a drag the camera no longer re-fits to a new scene or a resized surface, and a running flight ends.

For the person in front of it: widening a panel beside a graph rotates the graph.

## The Architectural Reality

- `Neo.app.SharedCanvas` forwards `down`, `up`, moves and `leave` to `renderer.updateMouseState`, each with the event's `buttons` (`src/app/SharedCanvas.mjs:372-438`).
- `Neo.canvas.Base#updateMouseState` copies `buttons` from every report into `mouse` and calls `onMouseDown` / `onMouseUp` for `down` / `up` (`src/canvas/Base.mjs:266-300`). `mouse.buttons` is the state of the device, not of this canvas.
- `Neo.canvas.GraphScene#updateMouseState` (`src/canvas/GraphScene.mjs:1371-1383`): `(mouse.buttons & 1) && (mouse.dx || mouse.dy)` adds to `camera.yaw` and `camera.pitch`, sets `camera.touched` and clears `flightPlan`.
- A `leave` resets `mouse` to idle. The first report after it moves by nothing, and the next one orbits again while a button is held.
- Spec surface: `test/playwright/unit/canvas/PointerInput.spec.mjs` (the base's pointer contract) and `test/playwright/unit/canvas/GraphScene.spec.mjs` (the camera).

## The Fix

The orbit belongs to a press the canvas received. `Neo.canvas.Base` is where "a drag gesture starts here" is written, so the base holds that fact: the buttons whose press reached this canvas and has not ended. It is set in `updateMouseState` before `onMouseDown` runs. It ends on `up`, on `leave`, and on a report whose `buttons` no longer carry the button. `GraphScene` orbits on that state instead of on `mouse.buttons`.

## Out of Scope

- Pointer capture, so that an orbit continues while the pointer is outside the canvas.
- Touch gestures.
- The Institution's splitter. It resizes correctly; a pointer that runs ahead of a splitter is how splitters behave.

## Avoided Traps

- **Fixing it in the app**, by having the canvas host drop `buttons` unless it saw the press: every `GraphScene` consumer has the same neighbours, and each app would write the base's contract again.
- **Slowing the test's drag** until no move lands on the canvas: the arm turns green and hands still hit it.
- **What the rule also did:** it carried an orbit back onto the canvas after the pointer had left with the button held. That stops, because the leave ends the press. Pointer capture is the way to keep such a drag, and it is out of scope here.
