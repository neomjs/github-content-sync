# Rotate Circle with a two-finger gesture

Owner: Euclid (`@neo-gpt-euclid`), accepting the Circle consumer handoff from the G2 steward. The shared recognizer remains in #22406.

## Context

A phone user turns Circle with two fingers. The ring follows the turn in either direction while the existing wheel and item drag still work. If a first finger has already started native scrolling, adding a second finger should leave Circle alone.

## The Problem

Circle currently responds to wheel deltas. Its mouse path already bounds `rotateZ`, updates `rotationIndex`, and redraws the rendered item window. The stock Main-to-App touch packet carries one contact’s movement. It cannot provide a signed two-contact turn. [Wheel path](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/blob/5ff17dedc8/src/component/Circle.mjs#L736) · [Touch forwarding](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/blob/5ff17dedc8/src/main/mixin/TouchDomEvents.mjs#L74)

On `5ff17dedc8` in mobile Chromium, either partial lift left the survivor’s next +4 px delta intact. A replacement pair added 24° to the earlier 48° without a jump. In the scroll-first control, Chromium emitted four two-contact moves after a trusted `pointercancel`. The unguarded probe turned Circle another 32° while the browser owned the sequence. [Real Circle browser receipt](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/discussions/22259#discussioncomment-19962152)

## The Architectural Reality

Main owns pair recognition and contact ownership. Circle consumes the ordinary App listener phases and owns its bounded angle, item index, and view. The browser retains a native scroll claim until all contacts lift. Compare Circle's scoped `pan-x pan-y` policy independently from global defaults. [G2](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22400) · [Decision handoff](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/discussions/22259#discussioncomment-19962093)

Decision Record: Required: ADR 0045, ticket #22401 and PR #22412.
Decision Record impact: aligned-with `adr:0045-touch-gesture-events`.

## The Fix

Subscribe Circle to the reviewed rotate phases. Apply the signed cumulative turn against the gesture’s starting angle and use the same angle bounds and item-index update as wheel input. Retire the anchor on end or cancel. Keep the ordinary item drag and wheel lifecycle. Use actual browser input on the shipped example to verify the consumer and scroll precedence.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] AC-1 The real Circle example responds to browser-generated turns in both directions from a non-boundary starting angle. Rendered angle and App item index agree, and the existing stop limits hold.
- [ ] AC-2 Either first lift leaves the surviving contact active and the rendered angle continuous. A replacement contact starts its pair from a fresh anchor. End, cancel, and teardown clear the App-side gesture state. Cancellation suppresses the gesture commit.
- [ ] AC-3 After a first finger starts native scrolling on Circle, a second contact on the same surface and subsequent two-contact moves leave Circle’s angle and item index unchanged. A fresh sequence after all contacts lift rotates successfully.
- [ ] AC-4 The scoped policy preserves native one-finger scroll on the surrounding surface. Run the Circle control on the supported device path, including physical iPhone behavior, and keep a browser-generated regression on the real example.
- [ ] AC-5 The wheel, existing visible controls and one-item drag still work. Rotation does not leave a drag proxy or start a competing drag when the recognizer owns the pair.
