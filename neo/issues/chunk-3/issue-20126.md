# A closed window's application stays alive in the App Worker

## Context

Measured while extending multi-window e2e coverage for a downstream dock application, and reproduced on `examples/dashboard/tearOut` (the dock's default tear-out route) at `dev` `056f5493c2`. Every window that departs leaves its `Neo.controller.Application` in `Neo.apps`, and its main view in the component manager, for as long as the SharedWorker lives.

The instrument was a CDP read of the App SharedWorker, after boot and after each pop-out round trip of the Notes pane (pop out, then close the vessel with its unload path):

| | `Object.keys(Neo.apps).length` | `Neo.manager.Component.items.length` |
|---|---|---|
| boot | 1 | 39 |
| round trip 1 | 2 | 41 |
| round trip 2 | 3 | 42 |
| round trip 3 | 4 | 43 |

The leaked entries are the closed vessels' apps (`Neo.examples.dashboard.tearOut`, one per window). In the downstream application the same mechanism also holds a warm-reloaded main window's previous app, together with whatever its shell kept: 13 components per reload, measured over three reloads with a vessel keeping the worker alive. Whatever such a tree registered (listeners, main-thread registrations keyed by its `windowId`) stays registered.

## The Problem

A departed window's id never comes back:
- `worker.Manager` mints `windowId` per document load: `crypto.randomUUID()`, `src/worker/Manager.mjs:108-112`.
- A page entering the bfcache never disconnects: the pagehide handler is `event.persisted || this.onBeforeUnload(event)`, `src/main/DomEvents.mjs:828-832`.

Nothing ever destroys that window's application:
- `worker.Base#onDisconnect` (`src/worker/Base.mjs:561-583`) retires the port and fires `disconnect`.
- `Neo.apps[windowId]` is deleted only by `controller.Application#destroy` (`src/controller/Application.mjs:158-169`), and nothing calls it on a disconnect.

So a long multi-window session grows by one app per closed popup and one per main-window reload, without bound.

## The Architectural Reality

Design authority: ADR 0029 §2, `learn/agentos/decisions/0029-docking-design.md:90`: *"A browser window — including the primary one — is a **render target**, never a state owner. The SharedWorker heap persists while at least one window remains connected; any single window (including the opener) can close or reload without destroying workspace truth."* A departed render target's application and main view own no truth.

What owners keep, they already move out on `disconnect`:
- The dock's Group detaches a released participant root inside `NativeLifecycle#withReleasedBinding` (a microtask after the release) via `NativeVesselTransaction.releaseWorkspace` → `native.detach(data)` (`src/dashboard/dock/window/NativeVesselTransaction.mjs:62-78`). It does this before any reconnect-lease wait, for both the `return` and the `retain` policy.
- The default tear-out route returns a vessel's pane through `TearOut#onBindingReleased` → `reintegrateItem` (`src/dashboard/dock/window/TearOut.mjs:563-690`).
- The worker guide, `learn/guides/fundamentals/WorkerArchitecture.md` ("Lifecycle Management"), shows apps adopting components on `connect`, but says nothing about what happens to a departed window's app.

## The Fix

Once a window has departed (its port retired in `worker.Base#onDisconnect`), the App Worker destroys `Neo.apps[windowId]` after the owners have had their turn, skipping an app that is already destroyed. Components moved out of the departed tree are untouched. Everything still inside it is reachable from no window, and goes with it.

Open for the design read before implementation:
1. **When.** The engine's own owners move synchronously or in a microtask. An app's own async `disconnect` handler may take longer. Options: a fixed bound equal to the Group's `reconnectLeaseMs` (the engine's longest disconnect deferral), a worker-level config, or the end of the disconnect's microtask queue plus a documented contract that owners move synchronously.
2. **Census** (`dev` `056f5493c2`). There are 11 `disconnect` listeners:
   - in `src/`: `code/LivePreview`, `dashboard/Container`, `manager/Window`, `manager/Transaction`, `ai/Client`;
   - in apps and examples: `onAppDisconnect` in portal, sharedcovid, shareddialog, `examples/stateProvider/multiWindow`, `multiWindowHelix` and `multiWindowCoronaGallery`.

   Each one moves what it keeps out of the departed tree. Three of them constrain the bound:
   - `LivePreview#onWindowDisconnect` reads `Neo.apps[windowId].mainView` inside the handler, so the reap must follow the handlers, never run with them.
   - `dashboard/Container#onWindowDisconnect` removes the widget synchronously, before its `await`.
   - The dock's `expired` path (`dashboard/dock/Workspace.mjs` → `TearOut#onBindingReleased`) returns a pane only after the Group's reconnect lease. Until then the pane can sit inside a departed vessel's tree, so any bound shorter than `reconnectLeaseMs` could destroy it mid-return. That favors option 1.
3. **Multi-app windows.** A disconnect naming one `appName` reaps that app only.

Decision Record impact: aligned-with `adr:0029-docking-design` (§2, a window is a render target).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] After a window departs (a closed popup, a closed or reloaded main window), its Application and main view are destroyed once the chosen bound passes, and `Neo.apps` holds the live windows only.
- [ ] A component moved out on `disconnect` survives the reap: a returned tear-out pane, a detached participant root, a retained headless workspace. Unit-tested.
- [ ] A disconnect naming one app of a multi-app window reaps only that app. Unit-tested.
- [ ] e2e on `examples/dashboard/tearOut`: three pop-out round trips leave `Neo.apps` at its boot count after the bound. Brain-free, so hosted CI selects it; worker truth comes over CDP.
- [ ] `WorkerArchitecture.md` "Lifecycle Management" states the contract: move what you keep on `disconnect`, and the departed window's application is destroyed after the bound.

## Out of Scope

- A reloaded vessel window that stays empty while its pane returns home (the default route reintegrates on release instead of re-adopting). Separate.
- Keeping components inside a departed tree on purpose: such an owner detaches them.
