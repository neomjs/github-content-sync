# Both contributors hear about an overlapping PR and its outcome

## Context

Source: [Discussion #21036](https://github.com/orgs/neomjs/discussions/21036), body `2026-11-15T20:33:46Z`, SHA-256 `fd18d5988a10a560a6cac874deb164ee03d37c76ae67a3feeec9a49ee737e813`. Two outside contributors opened PRs for the same curated TreeStore ticket. The later author learned that someone was already working on it. The earlier author only learned of the sibling when the maintainer retraced the case. Both public notices now exist. The pair is the rehearsal, outside the prospective sample. [Earlier PR](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/pull/21009#issuecomment-6900820364) · [Later PR](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/pull/21035#issuecomment-6897895590)

## The Problem

An author cannot plan their next step if only their counterpart hears about an overlap. A newcomer who has returned a repair can also lose a fair comparison when their CI needs maintainer approval. The team needs to find out whether clearly assigning the follow-up solves this without routine duplicate reviews. [Missed body correction](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/pull/20784#issuecomment-6813664183)

## The Architectural Reality

The issue’s linked PRs can be inspected today. Engine #21022 owns the incoming PR’s automatic notice, Brain #1631 owns sibling information in readiness, and #21024 owns a different measurement. The canonical maintainer wording is in the companion Skills leaf. #20895 and #21025 already touch contributor-facing text in the same guide.

## The Fix

Emmy records a prospective UTC start here before counting any pair. The current first-approve-worthy rule applies. Coordinate one contributor-facing sentence in the claim section with the existing guide work. For example, “If two pull requests cover the same fix, a maintainer will link them, tell both contributors who is coordinating and what happens next, and explain the choice to both before the competing fix lands.” The five-case read determines whether to retain or revise that practice.
