# Workstation and Fleet Manager wear their looks as palettes of theme Neo

Terminal predicate: Workstation, in light and in dark, and Fleet Manager each paint their look from one `Global.css` that re-values palette and role names of theme Neo; a floating component and a second window paint that look before and after a theme switch; neither product re-values a component variable that a role carries; and `check-theme-palette` measures each product palette as it measures a theme's.

Decision Record impact: amends `adr:0044-design-system`, decision 8, which hands this decision to the palette track. The amendment lands with the guard, in one pull request.

Source: questions 2 and 3 of [Discussion #22228](https://github.com/orgs/neomjs/discussions/22228), deferred at its graduation to this track, and the operator's direction of 27 Nov that both looks ship in 14.1 (#22391).

## Why an epic

One engine guard with its record amendment, a palette and a bridge removal in the Engine's Workstation, the same two in the Institution repository for Fleet, and one demonstration that gates the removals. Each waits for different package moves of #22295, and two repositories build.

## Problem scope

Read on `dev` at `28c9e53cf8` and in the Institution at `f3de9ffc90`.

1. **A product look is a bridge of component variables.** Workstation declares 15 names of its own (`--workstation-*`: eleven colours, two font stacks, two cursor values) per theme class in two projection files, and maps 96 component-variable declarations onto them in `resources/css/src/apps/workstation/Viewport.css`. Fleet does the same with `--fm-*` and `--agent-*` names. [The census of the Discussion](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/discussions/22228#discussioncomment-19931695) counted 171 and 150 overridden component variables, 54 of them the same, mapped onto about six slots: ground, panel, a second panel, line, ink, signal. Those six are roles now.
2. **The bridge stops at the viewport.** Its selector is the viewport and every theme-class element inside it. What floats is listed by name: the grid's drag proxy through `body:has(.workstation-viewport)` (`Viewport.css:58`), three menus and the splitter's proxy in Fleet (`agentos/Viewport.css:9` and `:946`). A floating component that nobody listed paints theme Neo, and so does a listed one in a window that never loads the sheet with its rule. Measured in the running app at `28c9e53cf8` ([Sophie's witness](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22365#issuecomment-7228773019)): the Close action's tooltip in a populated Metrics pop-out paints theme Neo's `#3e63dd` in both schemes, because Workstation's tooltip rule sits in `Workspace.css`, which that window does not load.
3. **A component can hold a stale theme, and what floats from it follows.** In the same witness the main window's tooltip stays light on a fresh hover after the viewport switched to dark. The tooltip reads its target's theme correctly; the tab header's toolbar and its Close action keep Light, while their tab container goes from no theme to Dark and the cascade updates only a child with no value or with the old one. Whatever carries a look, that tooltip paints the wrong scheme.
4. **The record left the mechanism open.** ADR 0044 decision 8: a palette is a set of palette and role values on a scope, the two looks become palettes, and how a palette reaches a floating component and a second window is decided by this track, whose entry gate is a demonstration with a theme switch and a secondary window. The deferral's reason was a palette designed against names about to change. The rename (`4d5236b292`) and theme Neo's `Global.css` (`255e64098f`) ended it.
5. **Today's guard refuses every product palette.** Planted into a copy of the tree, a second value for a palette or colour-role name of theme Neo fails `check-theme-palette` as `[cascade]` in an app's `Global.css`, in an app's `Viewport.css` and in a `src` sheet alike, and the same names under the viewport class fail as `[scope]`. The check holds a theme class to one palette and one value per name.

## Intended solution

**1. The carrier is the theme's own class.** A product palette is `resources/css/theme-<name>/apps/<app>/Global.css`. It declares palette and role names on the classes of the theme it builds on, light and dark in one file. Its folder puts it in `neo.apps`, above `neo.roles`, so its value wins on every element that carries the theme class, whichever sheet arrives first. A floating component with a theme stamps the class on itself (`afterSetTheme`, `src/component/Base.mjs:972`), which is the constraint the record wrote down. One created without a theme (a dialog, a toast, the grid's column menu) inherits from `body`, which carries its window's theme class (`src/main/addon/Stylesheet.mjs:74`, `src/container/Viewport.mjs:161`). Both resolve on a theme class, so both paint the palette.

Measured: each sheet through the build's `cascadeLayerPlugin` with the layer its path gets, read in Chromium. One widget is bound to roles through a `theme-base` sheet and sits at five places of a first document and in a second document whose viewport carries another app class. Each cell says whose value the widget paints.

| Where the product's values are declared | viewport | nested theme class | light island | inline-role scope | floating `body` child | second window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the theme's classes, in the app's `Global.css` | product | product | product | product | product | product |
| the app's viewport class | product | theme | theme | product | theme | theme |
| the viewport and the theme classes inside it (today's bridge) | product | product | product | product | theme | theme |
| a class on `body` and the theme classes inside it | product | product | product | product | product | product |

The rows read the same in dark and after the classes switch to light, and in both arrival orders. The scope keeps the role its inline style sets and takes the product's for every other.

**2. A product re-values names and adds none.** It may leave any name to its theme. The palette comes first: the roles read the palette on the same element, so a re-valued grade moves every role that reads it. A role is re-bound only where the look needs another grade. Within a palette a grade has one lightness in every slot (decision 2), so a product that moves a grade's lightness moves it in all seven slots, and one that keeps theme Neo's numbers re-values hue and chroma alone. A value between the product's grades is not a palette entry and not a role: it takes the nearest role with the shift named, or it stays the product's own variable that reads two roles, counted with its reason. Text maps by rank (one product name, one role), and a role's floor wins over reproduction. A grade is one value for all its readers: where a fill and a signal meet on one grade, the signal re-binds to a grade no role reads (Workstation's night signal reads `accent-300`, and `accent-200` stays the pale fill of a selected row). Decided on Workstation's eleven colours ([the read](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22432#issuecomment-7229571524)): its night look moves `950`, `900` and `800`, its day look keeps the ladder.

**3. The check measures a product palette.** `check-theme-palette` reads `theme-<name>/apps/<app>/Global.css` as a palette of its own: the theme's values with the product's on top. The names rule (it adds none), the two lightness rules and the contrast floors of both schemes hold for the merged values. Every other second declaration keeps failing as it does today. One app has one overlay per theme class: the file sits in the folder whose `Global.css` declares that class's palette, and an app's `Global.css` in a second folder mapped to the class fails (`.neo-theme-neo-dark` is declared by `theme-neo` and by `theme-neo-dark`, and the loader fetches from both). Run from a workspace, the check resolves the base through the build's roots. Both come from [the epic review](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22426#issuecomment-7229357029) and are criteria of #22427. This is also why the carrier is a class and not a selector with a condition: the check reads syntax alone and models no cascade, and a pair that only a browser can resolve is a pair no script measures.

**4. A window that runs the product loads the file.** The product's viewport class names the file in `additionalThemeFiles`; the loader fetches a named file per window (`insertThemeFiles`, `src/worker/App.mjs:535`). Fleet's pop-out host loads the shell's sheet by that route today. The limit is one product look per document.

**5. Each product owns its file; the Engine owns the names and the check.** Workstation's file sits in the Engine, Fleet's in the Institution's own `resources/css` root, which the theme build reads after the Engine's. No shared product base: the overlap the census counted is what the roles carry.

**6. What stays in a product's sheets.** Its structural rules, and a component variable it still re-values as a counted deviation with its reason (the tab header's two-panel ramp, the board's column width). Its own variables take the grammar and read roles (rule 12 of #22295). The names that held its colours go, and `check-theme-surfaces`, whose first half compares those names between two folders, is rewritten with the bridge's removal.

**7. The entry gate is a demonstration in the product.** A whitebox spec reads a role as a computed value on a floating component and inside a pop-out window, in dark and after a switch to light, the way `WorkstationPaletteBridgeNL` reads its tokens on their consumers. Its first cases are the two of Sophie's witness: the pop-out's tooltip, and the main window's tooltip after a switch, which needs the tab header to follow its container's theme. It lands with the palette file, and no bridge line goes before it is green. A case compares the painted value, not only the role on the element, so it depends on its widget's binding: the tooltip's background is a literal in theme Neo's light sheet until `tooltip` moves (#22295, step 6). Fleet's is the same spec in the Institution.

**8. Order against #22295.** The guard and the record need no package move. One sheet reads a colour role today, `src/Global.css`, so a palette file paints what that sheet paints and then each package as it moves. A bridge block goes when its package is in `theme-base`: `grid`, `tab`, `button`, `board`, `timeline`, `gantt`, `scheduler`, `tree`. The `workstation` row of step 6 is this epic's removal, not a row of its own. Fleet follows the Institution's pin bump onto the renamed Engine.

**Who decides what.** The acting design seat (Mnemosyne until Thu 3 Dec 21:00Z, then Clio) decides what these eight points leave open, asked on a leaf, and holds no build of this epic. A pull request that lands a decision of hers is reviewed by another seat, cross-family. Whether Workstation still looks like Workstation is read on the removal's frame pairs, and the operator merges.

**Open, each with its trigger.** Fleet's colours by model family, state and kind are categories, and ADR 0044 leaves a categorical scale out until a component needs one that reuse cannot give: Fleet's binding table was that trigger. Decided on 28 Nov ([the decision on Fleet's leaf](https://github.com/neomjs/neo-agent-institution/issues/1073#issuecomment-7230930270)): a category takes a role where it means that role; what no role means stays the product's own variable, which reads a role for lightness and chroma and names only its hue; identity and state keep their channels, rail and dot; no scale enters the Engine and no palette file declares a category. The hue form is computed and not yet built: its first arm is the leaf's. A second product look in one document, or a product as a nested root: see Out of scope.

## Out of scope

- **A palette class that travels beside the theme class.** Two product looks in one document, or a product nested as a root inside another app, need a class of their own that `component.Base` stamps wherever it stamps the theme class. That is an engine API. Neither product needs it, and the first host that does reopens it; a host that mounts two products one after the other and keeps the first one's sheet is such a host.
- **A generator for a product palette** and a workbench to tune one. `ROADMAP.md` lists the workbench after the migration.
- **The package moves themselves** (#22295) and the Material pair (#22359).
- **Peer-authored themes.** A full theme each, shaped in its own Discussion.

## Avoided traps

| Shape | Right when | Why not here |
|---|---|---|
| The app's viewport class, or the viewport and the theme classes inside it | Nothing of the product floats or opens a window. | Measured above: no floating component, and no second window whose viewport carries another class. It is why both products list floats by name today. |
| A class on `body` set in every window | A document has to load a product's sheets without wearing its look. | Same reach as the theme's class, but every window needs the class set, and its selector is a condition the palette check cannot read: the planted arm fails as `[scope]`. |
| A theme class of the product's own (`neo-theme-workstation-dark`) | The product has a design language of its own for every widget. | A product look called a theme is the trap #22295 names: a theme covers every widget in per-widget files. |
| The workspace root replacing `theme-neo/Global.css` at the same path | Never for a look. | It forks all 194 names to change a few, and the fork drifts with the first role the Engine adds. |
