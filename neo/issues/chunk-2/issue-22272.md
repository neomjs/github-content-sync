# Prevent a non-finite GridDragScroll fling when release samples share a timestamp

## Reproduction

[GridDragScroll](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/blob/0c1cc8eb58/src/main/addon/GridDragScroll.mjs#L140-L178) timestamps drag-history samples with `Date.now()`. When the oldest and newest samples in the release window have the same timestamp, the current calculation divides by zero. The animation then keeps scheduling frames because a non-finite velocity never satisfies the stop threshold.

Two controls reproduced this on `dev@0c1cc8eb58`:

- Calling the stock release and animation methods with two distinct positions at one millisecond timestamp produced infinite velocity on the moving axis and NaN on the other. After 500 manually pumped frames `activeDrag` was still live and another frame was queued.
- In an instrumented Chromium run on the stock Order Review Desk, a quick partial lift after a second contact armed produced a history with identical timestamps and positions. The release generated two NaN components; the tail still ran after 100 ms and `scrollTop` jumped from 78 to 0. The independent contact-ownership defect is #22271. Timing and actual device cadence affect reproducibility.

**Design authority:** the existing [kinetic scrolling feature](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/8916) and [cleanup contract](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/18104) require a finite, terminating tail after a valid release.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A release without a valid positive-duration velocity estimate leaves no kinetic frame or stale drag state and does not jump the grid. Check identical-timestamp samples with both equal and different positions.
- [ ] A legitimate positive-duration release still produces finite motion and settles. Exercise the actual addon methods, including the scheduled-frame queue and scroll position.
- [ ] Keep a refresh-rate-neutral momentum redesign as a separate decision in [the gesture sandbox](https://github.com/orgs/neomjs/discussions/22259). This fix should be verifiable without selecting a new friction curve or setting a phone performance target.
