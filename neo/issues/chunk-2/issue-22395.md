# Theme guards let a scale role be declared outside a Global.css

On the Engine `14.1` design-system critical path (#22295): step 6 moves 42 packages across many seats, and this guard holds rule 14 while they move. It carries no contributor labels for that reason.

unowned-rationale: measured and ready to build; offered to the maintainers by broadcast. The acting design seat keeps her week for the binding-table reads of step 6.

## Context

Rule 14 of epic #22295: a role and a palette name are declared in a `Global.css`; a component sheet declares component variables only. `check-theme-palette` landed with #22350 (`255e64098f`). Measured on `dev` at `5ff17dedc8`: one declaration is planted in a copy of `resources/css`, then each `check-theme-*` guard runs on that copy.

## The Problem

Four planted forms pass every theme guard:

| Planted | Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| A new scale role in a theme widget sheet, on a component selector | passes |
| The same, on a theme-class rule of the widget sheet | passes |
| An existing motion role re-valued in a `src` rule sheet | passes |
| A colour role under a condition nested inside the rule of a theme class without a palette | passes |

Controls on the same copy: a new colour role or a palette name in a `src` rule sheet fails as `[scope]`, and so does an existing colour role re-valued in a theme widget sheet. For a theme class without a palette, the unconditional colour role fails as `[scope]`, and the top-level `@media` form fails in `check-theme-value-files` as `[new-selector]`. The nested condition in a class with a palette fails as `[conditional]`.

A role declared in a widget sheet paints correctly, so nothing reports it. It then exists in one theme, `[names]` does not compare it, and the next theme written against the roles lacks it.

A second gap is in the tables that step 6 produces. Each binding table carries contrast ratios, and holders compute them by hand. Two of the five ratios checked in the first filed table were off (`1.39` for a measured `1.31`, `8.87` for `8.83`).

## The Architectural Reality

Design authority: ADR 0044, I5: "`check-theme-palette` compares the palette and role names each palette's `Global.css` declares, as `themeNameShapes` tells them, and fails one that a theme lacks or adds".

The guards know the kinds already. `kindOf(name)` in `buildScripts/util/themeNameShapes.mjs` tells palette, role, legacy and component names apart, and `[scope]` in `check-theme-palette.mjs` applies it to the palette and colour-role kinds. Two things are missing: the other role categories (`space`, `radius`, `duration`, `ease` and the rest), and the file. Nothing asks whether the declaring sheet is a `Global.css`. The palette check's summary keeps one freedom on purpose: "A scale role may be re-valued by a scope or a condition, as the reduced-motion collapse does."

The nested condition is the limit the reviewer accepted at the approval of #22352 ([note](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22295#issuecomment-7225947211)): a theme class without a palette is skipped, also for a name it declares under a condition. The note asks whoever touches the guard next to close it, or to narrow the module summary's sentence "it declares no colour role but the scheme".

`check-theme-palette.mjs` exports what a ratio needs: `readThemeDeclarations`, `resolveValue`, `parseColor` and `contrastRatio`.

## The Fix

1. A palette- or role-shaped name of any category is declared only in a `Global.css`: a theme's, or `src/Global.css`. A declaration in any other sheet fails with the sheet and the name. Inside a `Global.css` today's rules stay: a palette and a colour-role name sits on the theme class itself, under no condition, and a scope or a condition may re-value a scale role. `src/Global.css` declares a role only under a scope or a condition, as its reduced-motion collapse does (ADR 0044, decision 3); until #22394 lands, its seven unconditional motion roles are the one listed exception.
2. A palette or colour-role name under a condition fails for a theme class without a palette too, in the nested form as well.
3. The owner is the builder's choice between `[scope]` in `check-theme-palette.mjs` and `check-theme-property-prefix.mjs`, which already reads every engine sheet by kind. One rule, one failure class.
4. One command prints a pair's contrast in each scheme of a theme, with the guard's own functions: role or palette names in, the two ratios out, a translucent foreground composited on the background as the check does.

## Out of Scope

- The motion move and its guard in `check-theme-var-references` (#22394).
- Roles for `theme-dark`, `theme-light` and `theme-cyberpunk` (step 10 of the epic). The exemption for a theme without a palette stays until then.
- The pair `background-selected` on `background-accent-subtle`: it lands with the grid move (#22389).
- An app's or an example's own palette on a scope: the palette track decides how it declares roles (ADR 0044, decision 8).

## Avoided Traps

- **A second list of role names in the guard.** `themeNameShapes.mjs` owns the shapes, and the guards import it ([decided](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/22295#issuecomment-7222855183)).
- **Refusing by selector alone.** A widget sheet can declare on a theme class; the file decides, with the selector.
- **A ratio from the grade distance.** OKLCH lightness is not luminance; the command measures the pair, as the check does.
