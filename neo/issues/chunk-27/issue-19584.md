# Film close: tear the matrix out, grow its window, throw the grid, bring it home

## Context

The operator, 2026-10-11 ~01:05Z, on the v1 draft of the re-take at dev `00b6f2aa6a` (the first take whose close carries the sibling leaves' reveal and diagonal strokes): the drag-scroll inside the grid is far too slow, the grid's visible area in the room is tiny, he would have torn the matrix out and made the popup bigger (the film shows no window resize anywhere), and the drag-scroll should be fast and use the grid's own "throw" — on a fast release the grid keeps scrolling.

Design authority: the operator's design input (a peer design input, derived here: the hand-tempo rule sized the strokes like a walk, but a pan is a flick; the matrix at ≈ 300 px of width in the room can show neither its rows nor its canvas columns at scale; `GridDragScroll`'s momentum is the grid's own feature and the film never shows it); the 2026-09-23 pacing rule (a real gesture at hand tempo, never a held frame — a flick is a hand gesture; momentum is the content moving, not a held frame); the film's title claim ("the content never stopped living — because it never left") which the ending makes literal when the streaming window comes home.

## The Problem

The current `film-signature` scene (after the sibling leaves) brings the matrix and the feed in front, jumps the matrix to its midpoint, and pans it with three strokes of (−260, −120) at 36 samples × 33 ms — 1.2 s per stroke, ≈ 5 s in all — inside a pane ≈ 300 px wide. On film (take `2026-10-11T00-55-16-516Z`, the #15252 re-take comment) the close reads as a slow crawl in a small box: the rows are legible only in the pane's strip, the sparkline column enters at ≈ 93 s after three strokes, and nothing demonstrates the two things a desktop user would do with that grid — tear it out into a window of its own and flick it.

## The Architectural Reality

- Tear-out and native return exist as film cues: `apps/workstation/tour/fiveBeatFilm.mjs` `film-second-window` uses `{type: 'tear-out', itemId, sourceNodeId, options: filmPace}` (`NativeGestureDriver#executeTearOutStep`, the vessel born under the pointer beyond the window's bottom-right, the pane's footprint); `film-reintegration` uses `{type: 'native-return', ownerItemId, previewNodeId, options: {attempts, moveDelay, moveSteps}}` (`NativeGestureDriver#executeNativeReturnStep`, which resolves the vessel's owner-granted route via `Neo.manager.Window#resolveNativeRoute({capability: 'position', ownerWindowId, windowId})`, reads the frame with `Neo.Main.windowNativeGetGeometry`, and moves it with `Neo.Main.windowNativeMoveTo({...route, windowId: route.ownerWindowId, x, y})`, a verified outcome per step).
- Vessels are granted `{close: true, position: true, resize: true}` (`src/dashboard/dock/Workspace.mjs:1127`); `Neo.Main#windowNativeResizeTo({nativeHandleKey, targetWindowId, windowId, width, height})` (`src/Main.mjs:1244`) resizes the OUTER frame through the same owner grant and verifies `outerWidth/outerHeight` reached the request (`#resizeWindow`, `src/Main.mjs:974`); the dock's `NativeVesselTransaction` uses the move for the park re-show, nothing uses the resize yet. The display's available area is readable through `Neo.Main.getWindowData` (`screen.availLeft/Top/Width/Height`, `src/Main.mjs:343`).
- `GridDragScroll` (`src/main/addon/GridDragScroll.mjs`): the content follows the hand 1:1 during the drag; on release the velocity over the last 100 ms (px per 16 ms frame) decays with friction 0.95 until below 0.1 px/frame — a tail of ≈ 20 × v px, ≈ 2 s for a flick. `GestureDriver#executeGridDragScrollStep` already waits for that tail to settle and judges the travel against `min(requested, room)` (the #19580 Contract Ledger), so a throw's observed travel exceeds the request on the free axis and clamps at the room on the bounded one.
- The witness `test/playwright/e2e/workstation/WorkstationFilmTourNL.spec.mjs` asserts ≥ 4 popups born and all closed at the end; the signature's final `topology-assert` pins `split-main` = `['scale-tabs', 'heavy-tabs']` with `scale` active — both hold when the matrix window comes home before the readout.

## The Fix

Rewrite the `film-signature` steps after the two reveal ops (`scale-tabs → scale`, `bottom-tabs → feed`):

1. `tear-out` of `scale` from `scale-tabs` (existing cue): the matrix leaves the room, a window born under the hand, the same instance riding along.
2. **New cue `vessel-resize`** `{type: 'vessel-resize', itemId: 'scale', frame: {anchor: 'right-of-main', gap, width, height}, options: {moveDelay, moveSteps}}` → `NativeGestureDriver#executeVesselResizeStep`: resolves the vessel's owner route (capability `resize`, and `position` for the move), reads the frame, derives the target OUTER rect from the main window's frame (top-aligned, `gap` px right of its right edge; `height` defaults to the main frame's height; the display's available area from `getWindowData` clamps the target so a smaller display gets the biggest frame that fits, recorded in the proof), then walks position and size together from the birth frame to the target in `moveSteps` samples at `moveDelay` (`windowNativeMoveTo` + `windowNativeResizeTo` per sample, each a verified outcome; the film cursor is not involved — this is the window, not the hand). Receipt below. Listed in both cue vocabularies; `TourController#executeCue` routes it.
3. `scroll` to index 50000 (existing cue) — the midpoint jump, now in a window with room.
4. `grid-drag-scroll` as **one flick**: `{paneId: 'scale', anchor: {x: 0.8, y: 0.85}, deltaX: -360, deltaY: -480, strokes: 1, options: {moveDelay: 16, moveSteps: 6, attempts: 300, showCursor: true}}` — ≈ 130 ms of hand, then the grid's momentum carries the rows on for ≈ 2 s; the x axis clamps at the matrix's right room (the sparkline and action columns in view), the y axis runs well past the hand. The pane cache entry's `windowId` follows the pane into the vessel, so the existing executor dispatches into the popup window.
5. `native-return` of `scale` via `heavy-tabs` home to `scale-tabs` (existing cue): the window comes home still streaming.
6. The final `topology-assert` and the living close stay.

Captions name what each beat shows; the scene's `beats` list follows. `targetSeconds` stays a budget (10 s; the picture ≈ 12–14 s).

## Contract Ledger

**Cue** `{type: 'vessel-resize', itemId, frame, options?}` — consumed by `TourController#executeCue` → `NativeGestureDriver#executeVesselResizeStep({itemId, frame}, options)`; listed in `WORKSTATION_CUE_TYPES` and `filmCueTypes`.
- `itemId` (String, required): an item currently committed in its own vessel (the tear-out's receipt), else `applied: false` by name.
- `frame` (Object, required): `{anchor: 'right-of-main', gap: Number px (default 24), width: Number px (required), height: Number px (default: the main window's outer height)}` — the target OUTER frame, derived at run time from the main window's frame; clamped to the display's available area (`getWindowData`), the clamp recorded.
- `options.moveDelay` (ms between samples, default 33), `options.moveSteps` (samples, default 16).

**Receipt** `{applied, errors, proof}`; `proof = {before: {x, y, width, height}, target: {x, y, width, height, clamped: Boolean}, after: {x, y, width, height}, samples: Number, route: {windowId}}`.
- `applied: true` only when every sample's move and resize were verified by the main thread and the final frame is within 2 px of the target on every side.
- Failures by name: no committed vessel for the item; no owner-granted route; a refused sample (the frame as it stands in the proof); a final frame off the target.

**The throw** reuses `grid-drag-scroll`'s ledger (#19580): per axis `expected = min(requested, room)`, `observed ≥ expected − slack`; the flick adds nothing to the contract — the witness pins the momentum (`observed.y ≥ 2 × requested.y`) because that is what the beat claims.

**Decision Record impact:** aligned-with ADR 0029 (native window operations through the owner grant; no placement-rule change — the grow is a scripted frame operation like the park re-show, not a drag).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] `vessel-resize` is handled by `TourController#executeCue`, listed in both cue vocabularies, and the screenplay validates (`fiveBeatFilm.spec`); unit controls on `NativeGestureDriver.spec`: the frame walks to the derived target (move + resize per sample, verified), a display clamp is recorded and honoured, a refused sample fails by name with the frame as it stands, a missing vessel fails by name.
- [ ] The headless film witness: the `vessel-resize` receipt `applied` with `after` within 2 px of `target` and `after.width × after.height ≥ 4 ×` the pane's footprint before the tear-out; the throw's `grid-drag-scroll` receipt `applied`, `strokes: 1`, `after.settled`, `travel.observed.y ≥ 2 × travel.requested.y` (momentum), `travel.met` both axes; the `native-return` receipt `applied` with the matrix back in `scale-tabs`; ≥ 5 popups born, all closed; the final readout unchanged.
- [ ] The scene's picture at the headless clock: tear-out + grow + jump + flick + return ≈ 12–14 s; no sample slower than hand tempo, no held frame (the pause floors stay concurrent with their cues).
- [ ] Post-merge, deferred to #15252: the take's frames show the matrix window growing beside the room, the rows streaming after the flick with the sparkline column in frame, and the window flying home while the rows still move.

## Out of Scope

- Any engine change to the dock, the vessel birth size (the pane's footprint stays the product contract), the drag-scroll addon or its friction.
- The vessels of the journey scene and their choreography.
- Narration: A6 stays whole; the edit layer re-binds the close only.

## Avoided Traps

- Growing the vessel at birth (a product change to the dock's footprint contract) instead of a scripted window operation after it.
- Faking the throw by a `scroll` jump: the beat's claim is the grid's own momentum, measured by the receipt.
- Leaving the matrix window open at the end: it breaks the witness's born-and-closed invariant and the readout, and the title claim ("it never left") is weaker than the window coming home.
- Hard-coding the stage display's coordinates into the screenplay: the target frame derives from the main window and the display at run time.
