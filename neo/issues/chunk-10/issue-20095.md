# The reveal toolbar carries the revealed item's header actions

## Context

neomjs/neo-agent-institution#791 moves the Fleet Manager's Agent detail from a column that opens on a card selection to the right rail's held reveal (#20087), so a selection moves nothing. The design read on that ticket (Clio, [Q1 → (a)](https://github.com/neomjs/neo-agent-institution/issues/791#issuecomment-6388891885)) settles what the reveal's toolbar is: **the revealed item's header, so it carries the item's header actions — pin, pop-out, maximize where the item has them — under the docked header's gating, close excepted.** Today it carries the pin alone.

Measured on Institution `ada/791-detail-reveal` (headless Chromium, neo `dev` `d454606718`): the six Neural Link journeys that pop the detail out right after a selection fail until they pin it first. In `FleetCockpitPopOutNL`, `FleetCockpitDrillRoundTripNL` and `FleetPermanenceMatrixRow4NL` the revealed header has no pop-out action. In `FleetCockpitKineticNL`, `FleetCockpitNWindowNL` and `FleetPerspectiveCaptureVesseledNL` no popup opens, because the cockpit's `popOutPane('detail')` admits only the visible pane of a tab flow.

## The Problem

A person who reveals a railed item sees it, and "you pop out what you see" — but a revealed item can only be pinned. To pop it out they pin it (the column reflows), then use the docked header's pop-out: two steps and a reflow for one intent. An application holding a reveal for the person (the app-held reveal) has the same gap through the API.

## The Architectural Reality

- The reveal toolbar is built once in `src/dashboard/dock/interaction/RevealOverlay.mjs#construct`: a `TabHeaderToolbar` with one action, `pin` (`showOnFocus: false`), and the title button.
- Header truth is data: `src/dashboard/dock/projection/HeaderActionPolicy.mjs` publishes `dock.items.<itemId>.{closable, lockable, locked, pinnable, edge}` and `dock.popOutAvailable`, and binds each action's `hidden` / `pressed` / `disabled` formatter **closed over a tabs node's active item**. A revealed item is no tabs node's active item, so nothing binds it today.
- Commands route through `Workspace#onDockHeaderAction` to `src/dashboard/dock/plugin/HeaderActions.mjs`, whose handlers resolve the item and the pane rect from a `tabContainer` (`getActiveDockItemId`, `measureDockPaneRect`). The pop-out itself is item-based already: `handleDockPopOutAction` → `admitDockPopOut({itemId, proxyRect, sourceRect, sortZone})`, and `Operations.detachItem` takes a railed item and keeps `autoHidden` (ADR 0029 §2.7, detached row), so the item returns to its rail.

## The Fix

1. **RevealOverlay**'s toolbar carries the revealed item's header actions — pin, pop-out, maximize where the item has them, never close — each gated by the same policy truth the docked header reads, keyed on the revealed item instead of a tabs node.
2. **An item-keyed entry** into the header-action handlers for a revealed item: the pane rect is the reveal's pane slot, the item is the revealed one. Pop-out from a reveal ends the reveal and detaches the railed item through the existing admission path.
3. **ADR 0029 §2.7**: one row, phrased as the rule — the reveal toolbar is the revealed item's header and carries its header actions under the docked header's gating, close excepted. "The dock header owns the ONE pop-out verb" still holds: an item has one header, and while revealed that header is the reveal toolbar.
4. **The reveal's end says why.** Today `dockRailRevealChange` reports only the next and previous snapshots, so an application that clears its selection when the person dismisses a held reveal cannot tell a pop-out from Escape. It would deselect the resident just as its window opens (Maryam's depth-floor challenge on neomjs/neo-agent-institution#805, review 5640152897). The relayed change names the end's cause: the person (Escape, the item's own tab), the application's release, or the item leaving the rail (a pin or a pop-out). The Institution can then drop the flag that marks its own dismissals.

Decision Record impact: amends ADR 0029 §2.7 (the reveal carries more than the pin).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] AC-1 (browser, red on `dev` sources): a revealed item that the docked header would offer a pop-out shows a pop-out in its reveal toolbar; using it opens the item in its own window, and closing that window returns it to its rail, still `autoHidden`.
- [ ] AC-2: the reveal toolbar shows exactly the actions the docked header would show for the item, under the same gating (a `pinnable: false` item shows no pin; no pop-out where `dock.popOutAvailable` is false), and never close.
- [ ] AC-3: an app-held reveal pops out through the same entry, and the workspace's `dockRailRevealChange` reports the reveal's end.
- [ ] AC-4: the ADR 0029 §2.7 row and the Status line's amendment entry.
- [ ] AC-5 (unit): every `dockRailRevealChange` that ends a reveal names its cause. A pop-out or a pin from an app-held reveal reports the item leaving the rail, never a person's dismissal; Escape and the item's tab report the person; `Workspace#dismissRailReveal` reports the application. A consumer that reads no cause behaves as today.

## Out of Scope

The Fleet Manager's own journeys: neomjs/neo-agent-institution#791 pins first in the six journeys above (its fixtures' `pinRevealedDetail`) until this lands. A follow-up there deletes the helper and its calls, reads the end's cause in `CockpitPerspectives.detailRevealChange` instead of the cockpit's own flag, and keeps the resident selected through a pop-out. Center items never rail, so nothing changes for them.

## Avoided Traps

Pin first, then pop out (Clio's Q1 (b)): it adds a reflow to a product path. A second pop-out verb beside the docked one: two verbs for one intent, against ADR 0029's one-header rule.
