# Find what made the 100k Matrix pane content-sized before the tab crash

## Context

Found in manual testing of `apps/workstation` on `dev` (2026-10-22). After a few layout steps, the 100k Matrix pane ran past the window's right edge. The App worker logged an endless `[Row, Row]` duplicate-id loop, and Chrome climbed to about 160 % CPU before the tab crashed ("Aw, Snap!").

#19991 fixed the two engine defects behind the crash (#19998, merged):
- an unbounded view measured the body's full 5,000,050 px scroll height, so `createRowPool` built 99,970 rows;
- `items.push(...newRows)` threw a `RangeError` after every row had registered its id.

It left the **trigger** out of scope: what made the Matrix pane's chain content-sized in the first place. With #19998 in, a forced content-sized pane no longer hangs the worker. The trigger itself is still unexplained.

## Known so far

- **The three committed steps** ("Undo 3") are encoded in the layout restored after the crash: Live Event Stream dropped on the bottom root edge (`split-root-0`), Evidence Audit split before it (`split-tabs-feed-0-0`), and Resident Activity Timeline moved into that pane (`tabs-audit-0`).
- **The operator's account:** "moving a grid was certainly involved". That was most likely a fourth action, which never committed because the worker wedged mid-drop.
- **Headless replays on `dev` stayed healthy** at the operator's 1278×1017 window. In every one the worker answered, no duplicate ids appeared, and the Matrix pane stayed inside the dock host:
  - the restored document applied in one shot;
  - the three operations as three separate Group commits;
  - a real-pointer drag of the Matrix tab across six waypoints, pinging the worker during the drag.

  Headless pointer drops into nested split zones were unreliable, so not every real-gesture variant ran.
- **Hypothesis from the operator:** it is related to the OffscreenCanvas cells. Every Matrix row carries a "Living signal" sparkline, an OffscreenCanvas cell registered in the Canvas worker, so 99,970 rows mean as many canvas registrations. A known latent hazard (#19110): handing an already-transferred canvas over again makes `transferControlToOffscreen` throw silently, and the duplicate-id loop re-created rows whose canvases were already registered. It is open whether the canvas cells are only the amplifier of the crash or also part of its trigger.

## The Architectural Reality

- `src/grid/Body.mjs`: `createRowPool()` and the row ids (`getRowId`).
- The sparkline cells: `src/component/Sparkline.mjs` (App worker) and `src/canvas/Sparkline.mjs` (the Canvas worker renderer). The OffscreenCanvas handover goes through `Neo.main.DomAccess`.
- The dock projection that sizes the pane chain during a drag and drop.

## Investigation

1. A live repro with the operator, recording the screen, while the session is read over the Neural Link, read-only. Capture the History rows, the pane chain's computed sizes, and the Canvas worker's registration count before the worker goes deaf.
2. If it reproduces, bisect the pane chain to the first ancestor that loses its definite size, and the operation that made it lose it.
3. Measure the canvas-cell side directly: registrations per created row, and whether a re-created row re-transfers its canvas.
