# Grid: a 100k wrapped-text example proves content-sized rows end to end

## Context

This is leaf L6 of epic #19658, the acceptance journey. It is blocked by #19662 and #19663 (measurement), #19664 (scroll stability) and #19665 (`AnimateRows`). The epic's predicate is a user outcome: a buffered grid opts into content-sized rows, the visible record stays stable, bodies stay aligned, and keyboard navigation and the final row work. Only a real example plus a whitebox e2e proves it. D#19645's probe 6 estimated the measurement load from a fixed-height grid; this leaf measures the integrated implementation.

## The Fix

- **The example:** an example app (placement settled at intake, next to `examples/grid/bigData`) with 100k flat records whose text columns wrap one to four lines, at least one declared wrapping column in each of the start, center and end bodies (locked columns), content-sized mode on, and a fixed-height toggle as the control.
- **The e2e:** a whitebox e2e over D#19645's ten required examples:
  1. an initially unmounted tallest column;
  2. a width change and a font change;
  3. edit, sort and filter;
  4. delayed recycled responses;
  5. reverse wheel scrolling;
  6. thumb drag and release;
  7. jump-to-row;
  8. the final row, reachable and fully visible;
  9. two views at different widths;
  10. the unchanged fixed-height control.
- **The throughput measurement:** under real wheel input at ordinary and fast speeds, record the rows entering per second, the share of rows mounted at the estimate, the main-thread time spent measuring per second, and frames over 20 ms. Compare them against probe 6's fixed-height baseline (41–71 rows/s, 50–80 ms to fresh rows, 0 long frames).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] The example runs with 100k wrapped-text rows across three bodies, and its fixed-height toggle shows unchanged fixed behaviour.
- [ ] The whitebox e2e covers all ten examples above, each as a named test.
- [ ] The integrated wheel-throughput numbers (rows entering per second, share mounted at the estimate, measuring time per second, long frames) are recorded in the PR against probe 6's baseline, with no frames over 20 ms at ordinary wheel speed.
